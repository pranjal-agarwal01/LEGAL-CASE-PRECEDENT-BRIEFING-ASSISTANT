## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] Donoghue v Stevenson [1932] AC 562 supports Priya Nair on this issue: Lord Atkin's neighbour principle: you must take reasonable care to avoid acts or omissions which you can reasonably foresee would be likely to injure your neighbour, meaning persons so closely and directly affected by your act that you ought reasonably to have them in contemplation. That reasoning applies here: The bottle was dark brown glass and was still sealed with Brightwater's crimped metal cap when it was opened at the table, so the contents could not be seen or inspected. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2
- **A1.2** [I1] Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police [2018] UKSC 4 supports Priya Nair on this issue: Caparo did not lay down a universal three-stage test to be applied in every case. That reasoning applies here: On 14 March 2025 Arjun Mehta bought two bottles of Fizzleaf kombucha at the Green Cup cafe in Leeds and gave one of them to Ms Nair. — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1
- **A1.3** [I2] Chester v Afshar [2004] UKHL 41 supports Priya Nair on this issue: On policy grounds, causation may be established by a narrow modification of traditional principles in order to vindicate the patient's right of autonomy and to give effect to the duty to warn of material risks before consent to surgery. That reasoning applies here: Brightwater says its bottling line passed a safety inspection on 1 March 2025 and suggests the cafe may have tampered with the bottle. Its medical expert says that the ulcer, not the glass, caused most of the damage and that... — *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7
- **A1.4** [I3] Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd [1962] 2 QB 405 supports Priya Nair on this issue: The eggshell skull rule: once the type of harm is foreseeable, the tortfeasor is liable for its full extent even if that extent is greater than could have been foreseen because of the claimant's pre-existing physical vulnerability or condition. That reasoning applies here: Ms Nair has a pre-existing gastric ulcer condition. Her consultant says that the condition made the internal injury far more serious than it would have been for a healthy person. — *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A1.5** [I3] Page v Smith [1996] AC 155 supports Priya Nair on this issue: A primary victim who is directly involved and within the zone of foreseeable physical danger need not satisfy the control mechanisms applied to secondary victims, and the defendant takes the victim as found, including a pre-existing psychological vulnerability. That reasoning applies here: She was unable to work until 30 June 2025 and needed two further procedures. — *Page v Smith* [1996] AC 155, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6
