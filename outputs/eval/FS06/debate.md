# Debate transcript: Adeyemi v Harbourside Hotel Ltd

Mode: offline · LLM calls: 0

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd [1949] 1 KB 532 supports Folake Adeyemi on this issue: Terms must be brought to a party's notice before or at the time the contract is made; a notice first seen after the contract has been concluded is not incorporated. That reasoning applies here: A notice on the back of her bedroom door stated: "The hotel accepts no liability for loss of or damage to guests' property left in rooms. Valuables must be deposited at reception.". — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A1.2** [I1] Parker v South Eastern Railway Co (1877) 2 CPD 416 supports Folake Adeyemi on this issue: Unsigned terms on a ticket or notice are incorporated if the party relying on them took reasonable steps to bring them to the other party's attention before or at the time the contract was made. That reasoning applies here: At reception she signed a registration card recording her name, address and car registration. The card contained no terms and conditions and did not refer to any. She paid for two nights and was given a key card. — *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd [1980] AC 827 supports Harbourside Hotel Ltd on this issue: In commercial contracts between parties of equal bargaining power who allocate risks, including by insurance, a clearly worded exclusion or limitation clause will be given effect according to its true construction. That reasoning applies here: The hotel relies on the bedroom notice to exclude liability and says Ms Adeyemi should have deposited her valuables at reception. — *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C1.2** [I1] L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd [1934] 2 KB 394 supports Harbourside Hotel Ltd on this issue: A party who signs a contractual document is bound by its terms in the absence of fraud or misrepresentation, regardless of whether they read them. That reasoning applies here: At reception she signed a registration card recording her name, address and car registration. The card contained no terms and conditions and did not refer to any. She paid for two nights and was given a key card. — *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd is distinguishable on its facts: Securicor contracted to provide night patrols at Photo Production's factory for a modest charge. By contrast, in this case: The hotel relies on the bedroom notice to exclude liability and says Ms Adeyemi should have deposited her valuables at reception. — *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶1; Fact sheet ¶6
- **A2.2** [I1] *(replies to C1.2)* L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd is distinguishable on its facts: Miss L'Estrange bought an automatic vending machine for her cafe and signed an order form containing, in small print, a clause excluding all express and implied warranties. By contrast, in this case: On 12 August 2025 Ms Adeyemi arrived at the Harbourside Hotel without a booking and asked for a room at the reception desk. — *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶1; Fact sheet ¶1
- **A2.3** [I1] Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd [1971] 2 QB 163 supports Folake Adeyemi on this issue: Terms referred to on a ticket issued after the contract is concluded are not incorporated, and the more onerous or unusual a clause is, the more clearly it must be brought to the other party's attention. That reasoning applies here: The hotel relies on the bedroom notice to exclude liability and says Ms Adeyemi should have deposited her valuables at reception. — *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6
