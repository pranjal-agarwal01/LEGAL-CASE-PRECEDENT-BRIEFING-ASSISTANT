## Summary

- Acting for Megan Hughes (claimant), this brief addresses 2 issue(s) arising from Hughes v PeakFit Ltd: Ms Hughes wants to enforce PeakFit's promise to pay £2,000 as a binding contract and claim the money. — Fact sheet ¶7
- Did PeakFit Ltd intend to create legal relations, or was the promise a mere puff? Favourable for Megan Hughes. — *Merritt v Merritt* [1970] 1 WLR 1211, ¶3; *Balfour v Balfour* [1919] 2 KB 571, ¶3
- Was PeakFit Ltd's communication a binding offer, rather than an invitation to treat, which Megan Hughes accepted? Unfavourable for Megan Hughes. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶3

## Issues

1. **I1.** Did PeakFit Ltd intend to create legal relations, or was the promise a mere puff?
2. **I2.** Was PeakFit Ltd's communication a binding offer, rather than an invitation to treat, which Megan Hughes accepted?

## Key facts

- On 5 January 2025 PeakFit published an advertisement on its website and social media: "Complete all 100 daily PeakFit workouts by 15 April and if you have not lost 5 kg we will pay you £2,000. We have placed £100,000 with Northgate Bank to show we mean it." — Fact sheet ¶1
- The advertisement said participants must log every workout in the app and weigh in on the app's connected scales on 15 April. — Fact sheet ¶2
- Ms Hughes downloaded the app on 6 January 2025, paid the £9.99 subscription, logged all 100 workouts and weighed in on 15 April 2025, having lost only 1.2 kg. — Fact sheet ¶3
- On 20 April 2025 she emailed PeakFit claiming £2,000. PeakFit replied that the advertisement was a marketing promotion and only an invitation to treat, that it never accepted her as a participant, and that she should have told it when she started. — Fact sheet ¶4
- PeakFit's general terms say that promotions "may be withdrawn at any time". PeakFit did not withdraw this promotion before 15 April 2025. — Fact sheet ¶5
- More than 3,000 people downloaded the app during the promotion. PeakFit says it never expected to be legally bound to pay all of them and that the advertisement was obvious marketing puff. — Fact sheet ¶6
