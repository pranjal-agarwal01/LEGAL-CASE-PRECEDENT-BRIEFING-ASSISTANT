## Issues

1. **I1.** Is Castlegate Developments Ltd's promise of additional payment supported by consideration?

## Key facts

- In January 2025 Castlegate engaged Brennan to fit kitchens and staircases in 30 new townhouses for a fixed price of £150,000, to be completed by 30 June 2025. — Fact sheet ¶1
- Castlegate's own contract with the developer contains a liquidated damages clause of £5,000 per day for late completion. — Fact sheet ¶2
- By April 2025 Brennan had completed 12 houses. It told Castlegate that it was running out of money because the price was too low and that it might not be able to finish. — Fact sheet ¶3
- On 14 April 2025 Castlegate's project director promised by email to pay Brennan an extra £1,000 for each remaining house completed on schedule, "so we don't get hit with the developer's penalties or have to find another joiner". — Fact sheet ¶4
- Brennan completed the remaining 18 houses by 30 June 2025. Castlegate paid the original contract price but refused to pay the extra £18,000, saying Brennan was already obliged under the existing contract to do that work. — Fact sheet ¶5
- There is no suggestion that Brennan threatened to walk away to obtain the promise; the offer of extra payment came from Castlegate. — Fact sheet ¶6

## I1. Is Castlegate Developments Ltd's promise of additional payment supported by consideration?

**Supporting precedents**

|---|---|---|
| *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1 | Where A promises B additional payment for performing an existing contractual obligation, and A obtains a practical benefit or obviates a disbenefit, that benefit can be good consideration provided the promise was not extracted by economic duress or fraud. | *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3 |

- Stilk v Myrick is distinguishable on its facts: During a voyage two seamen deserted.
