# Debate transcript: Brennan Joinery Ltd v Castlegate Developments Ltd

Mode: offline · LLM calls: 0

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd [1991] 1 QB 1 supports Brennan Joinery Ltd on this issue: Where A promises B additional payment for performing an existing contractual obligation, and A obtains a practical benefit or obviates a disbenefit, that benefit can be good consideration provided the promise was not extracted by economic duress or fraud. That reasoning applies here: Brennan completed the remaining 18 houses by 30 June 2025. Castlegate paid the original contract price but refused to pay the extra £18,000, saying Brennan was already obliged under the existing contract to do that work. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A1.2** [I1] Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd [1947] KB 130 supports Brennan Joinery Ltd on this issue: Promissory estoppel: a promise intended to be binding, intended to be acted on and in fact acted on is binding so far as its terms properly apply, even without consideration, and it may be suspensory once the circumstances change. That reasoning applies here: On 14 April 2025 Castlegate's project director promised by email to pay Brennan an extra £1,000 for each remaining house completed on schedule, "so we don't get hit with the developer's penalties or have to find another joiner". — *Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd* [1947] KB 130, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] Stilk v Myrick (1809) 2 Camp 317 supports Castlegate Developments Ltd on this issue: Performance of an existing contractual duty owed to the promisor is not good consideration for a promise of additional payment. That reasoning applies here: Brennan completed the remaining 18 houses by 30 June 2025. Castlegate paid the original contract price but refused to pay the extra £18,000, saying Brennan was already obliged under the existing contract to do that work. — *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
- **C1.2** [I1] Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd [1947] KB 130 supports Castlegate Developments Ltd on this issue: Promissory estoppel: a promise intended to be binding, intended to be acted on and in fact acted on is binding so far as its terms properly apply, even without consideration, and it may be suspensory once the circumstances change. That reasoning applies here: On 14 April 2025 Castlegate's project director promised by email to pay Brennan an extra £1,000 for each remaining house completed on schedule, "so we don't get hit with the developer's penalties or have to find another joiner". — *Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd* [1947] KB 130, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* Stilk v Myrick is distinguishable on its facts: During a voyage two seamen deserted. By contrast, in this case: There is no suggestion that Brennan threatened to walk away to obtain the promise; the offer of extra payment came from Castlegate. — *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶1; Fact sheet ¶6
