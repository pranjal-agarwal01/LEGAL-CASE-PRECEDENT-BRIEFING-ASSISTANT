# Debate transcript: Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd v SwiftHaul Couriers Ltd

Mode: offline · LLM calls: 0

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd [1949] 2 KB 528 supports Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd on this issue: Recoverable loss is that which was reasonably foreseeable as liable to result from the breach, judged by the knowledge the defendant possessed at the time of contracting, whether imputed knowledge of the ordinary course of things or actual knowledge of special circumstances. That reasoning applies here: When booking, the bakery's manager ticked a box confirming acceptance of SwiftHaul's terms and conditions. Clause 9 excludes liability "for any loss of profit, business or contracts, however caused, arising from delay". — *Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd* [1949] 2 KB 528, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A1.2** [I2] Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd [1949] 1 KB 532 supports Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd on this issue: Terms must be brought to a party's notice before or at the time the contract is made; a notice first seen after the contract has been concluded is not incorporated. That reasoning applies here: When booking, the bakery's manager ticked a box confirming acceptance of SwiftHaul's terms and conditions. Clause 9 excludes liability "for any loss of profit, business or contracts, however caused, arising from delay". — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A1.3** [I2] Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd [1971] 2 QB 163 supports Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd on this issue: Terms referred to on a ticket issued after the contract is concluded are not incorporated, and the more onerous or unusual a clause is, the more clearly it must be brought to the other party's attention. That reasoning applies here: SwiftHaul says it could not have contemplated that the bakery would be unable to trade, that the wedding contract was exceptional, and that in any event clause 9 excludes all of these losses. — *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] Hadley v Baxendale (1854) 9 Exch 341 supports SwiftHaul Couriers Ltd on this issue: Damages for breach of contract are recoverable for loss arising naturally, according to the usual course of things, from the breach, or for loss that may reasonably be supposed to have been in the contemplation of both parties at the time they made the contract as the probable result of its breach. That reasoning applies here: SwiftHaul says it could not have contemplated that the bakery would be unable to trade, that the wedding contract was exceptional, and that in any event clause 9 excludes all of these losses. — *Hadley v Baxendale* (1854) 9 Exch 341, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* Hadley v Baxendale is distinguishable on its facts: The crankshaft of the claimants' mill broke. By contrast, in this case: The bakery could not bake for twelve days. It lost ordinary trade profits of about £18,000, and lost a one-off contract worth £40,000 to supply cakes for a celebrity wedding, which SwiftHaul knew nothing about. — *Hadley v Baxendale* (1854) 9 Exch 341, ¶1; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A2.2** [I2] *(replies to C1.3)* Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd is distinguishable on its facts: Securicor contracted to provide night patrols at Photo Production's factory for a modest charge. By contrast, in this case: The bakery could not bake for twelve days. It lost ordinary trade profits of about £18,000, and lost a one-off contract worth £40,000 to supply cakes for a celebrity wedding, which SwiftHaul knew nothing about. — *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶1; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A2.3** [I2] *(replies to C1.4)* L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd is distinguishable on its facts: Miss L'Estrange bought an automatic vending machine for her cafe and signed an order form containing, in small print, a clause excluding all express and implied warranties. By contrast, in this case: Through SwiftHaul's error the parcel was sent to the wrong depot and was delivered on 15 September 2025, eleven days late. — *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶1; Fact sheet ¶4
- **A2.4** [I2] Parker v South Eastern Railway Co (1877) 2 CPD 416 supports Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd on this issue: Unsigned terms on a ticket or notice are incorporated if the party relying on them took reasonable steps to bring them to the other party's attention before or at the time the contract was made. That reasoning applies here: The bakery could not bake for twelve days. It lost ordinary trade profits of about £18,000, and lost a one-off contract worth £40,000 to supply cakes for a celebrity wedding, which SwiftHaul knew nothing about. — *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
