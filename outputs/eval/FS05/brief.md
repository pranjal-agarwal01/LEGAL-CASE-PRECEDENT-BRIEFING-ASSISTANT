## Issues

1. **I1.** Are Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd's claimed losses within what the parties reasonably contemplated when they contracted?
2. **I2.** Can SwiftHaul Couriers Ltd rely on the exclusion clause: was it incorporated into the contract and does it cover the loss?

## Key facts

- On 3 September 2025 the controller board of the bakery's only industrial oven failed. The manufacturer agreed to repair it if it was delivered to its workshop in Birmingham. — Fact sheet ¶1
- On 4 September 2025 the bakery booked SwiftHaul's guaranteed next-day delivery service online for £45, describing the parcel only as "electronic component". It did not tell SwiftHaul that the bakery could not trade without it. — Fact sheet ¶2
- When booking, the bakery's manager ticked a box confirming acceptance of SwiftHaul's terms and conditions. Clause 9 excludes liability "for any loss of profit, business or contracts, however caused, arising from delay". — Fact sheet ¶3
- Through SwiftHaul's error the parcel was sent to the wrong depot and was delivered on 15 September 2025, eleven days late. — Fact sheet ¶4
- The bakery could not bake for twelve days. It lost ordinary trade profits of about £18,000, and lost a one-off contract worth £40,000 to supply cakes for a celebrity wedding, which SwiftHaul knew nothing about. — Fact sheet ¶5
- SwiftHaul says it could not have contemplated that the bakery would be unable to trade, that the wedding contract was exceptional, and that in any event clause 9 excludes all of these losses. — Fact sheet ¶6

## I2. Can SwiftHaul Couriers Ltd rely on the exclusion clause: was it incorporated into the contract and does it cover the loss?
