## Key facts

- On 21 November 2024 a Dunmore lorry driver who was using his phone crashed into the back of a car driven by 19-year-old Jamie Clarke on the M62 motorway. — Fact sheet ¶1
- Jamie suffered severe head and leg injuries and was taken by ambulance to Hull Royal Infirmary. — Fact sheet ¶2
- Helen Clarke was told of the crash by a police officer at her home about one hour later. She arrived at the hospital roughly two hours after the crash and saw Jamie in the emergency department before he had been cleaned up, covered in blood and unconscious. — Fact sheet ¶3
- Ryan Clarke, at work in Leeds, watched the aftermath of the crash on a live traffic-camera stream on a news website. He recognised Jamie's car but could not see Jamie himself. — Fact sheet ¶4
- Both Helen and Ryan have since been diagnosed with post-traumatic stress disorder, a recognised psychiatric illness. Jamie survived. — Fact sheet ¶5
- Dunmore admits that its driver was negligent towards Jamie but denies owing any duty of care to Helen or Ryan for psychiatric harm. — Fact sheet ¶6

## I1. Can Helen Clarke and Ryan Clarke recover for psychiatric injury, and as a primary or a secondary victim?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *McLoughlin v O'Brian* [1983] 1 AC 410 | A close relative who does not witness the accident may recover if they come upon its immediate aftermath and perceive it directly. | *McLoughlin v O'Brian* [1983] 1 AC 410, ¶3 |
| *Page v Smith* [1996] AC 155 | A primary victim who is directly involved and within the zone of foreseeable physical danger need not satisfy the control mechanisms applied to secondary victims, and the defendant takes the victim as found, including a pre-existing psychological vulnerability. | *Page v Smith* [1996] AC 155, ¶3 |
