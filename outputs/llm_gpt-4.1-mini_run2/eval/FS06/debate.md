# Debate transcript: Adeyemi v Harbourside Hotel Ltd

Mode: llm:foundry · LLM calls: 9

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] The exclusion notice on the bedroom door was not incorporated into the contract because the contract was made at reception when Ms Adeyemi signed the registration card without any terms or references to conditions, and the notice was only visible later in the bedroom; this follows Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd, which held such after-contract notices are not part of the contract. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A1.2** [I2] The hotel owed Ms Adeyemi a duty of care to safeguard her belongings as she was a hotel guest and it was reasonably foreseeable her property could be at risk, aligning with the principles in Donoghue v Stevenson that impose a duty to avoid foreseeable harm. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A1.3** [I3] The exclusion notice is not valid or enforceable against Ms Adeyemi because it was neither incorporated into the contract nor clearly communicated before the contract was made, consistent with the ruling in Olley that exclusion clauses must be brought to notice pre-contract. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6
- **A1.4** [I4] Ms Adeyemi acted reasonably in not depositing her valuables at reception, as the burden was on the hotel to provide adequate security and clear contractual terms before contract formation, not on the guest to anticipate risks without contractual notice, supported by the principle in Parker v South Eastern Railway, requiring reasonable notice of terms. — *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] *(replies to A1.1)* The exclusion notice on the bedroom door was not incorporated into the contract because the contract was completed at reception at the point of signing the registration card, where no reference was made to any terms or conditions, similar to the facts in Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd where the bedroom notice could not be relied upon as it came after the contract formation as the hotel was negligent with the room key (J42:p1, J42:p2, J42:p3; F2, F3, F5). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶1; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C1.2** [I2] *(replies to A1.2)* The hotel did not owe a duty of care to Ms Adeyemi in the way claimed as the relationship here is a contract for accommodation, not a positive act causing physical injury as in Robinson or manufacture of goods as in Donoghue; thus no novel duty arises and the contractual allocation of risk controls liability (J01:p2; F5). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
- **C1.3** [I3] *(replies to A1.3)* The exclusion notice should be held valid and enforceable because the hotel took reasonable steps to bring the terms to the guest's attention, akin to Parker where reasonable notice of terms via a ticket was sufficient to incorporate them (J41:p1, J41:p3; F3, F6). Furthermore, clear and commercially reasonable exclusion clauses like in Photo Production Ltd v Securicor are upheld even for fundamental breaches if properly worded (J44:p2). — *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶1; *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶3; *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C1.4** [I4] *(replies to A1.4)* Ms Adeyemi failed to act reasonably by not depositing valuables at reception where the notice clearly stated such a requirement; the hotel is not liable for negligent acts arising from the guest's failure to follow reasonable security measures provided, as in Parker where actual notice of terms was decisive (J41:p1, J41:p3; F3, F6). — *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶1; *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* The exclusion notice on the bedroom door was not incorporated into the contract because the contract was completed at reception when Ms Adeyemi signed the registration card without any reference to terms, similar to Olley where the after-contract bedroom notice was held not part of the contract (J42:p2; F2, F3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A2.2** [I2] *(replies to C1.2)* The hotel owed Ms Adeyemi a duty of care for her belongings as a hotel guest, given the foreseeable risk of theft within the premises, consistent with Donoghue establishing a duty to take reasonable care to avoid foreseeable harm to neighbours including guests (J01:p2; F5). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A2.3** [I3] *(replies to C1.3)* The exclusion notice is invalid and unenforceable since it was not incorporated as it came after contract formation and was not brought to Ms Adeyemi's notice before or at that time, per Olley requiring pre-contract notice for exclusion clauses (J42:p3; F2, F3). The hotel did not take reasonable steps before contract to incorporate the term, unlike Parker's reasonable steps which were absent here (J41:p3; F2). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2
- **A2.4** [I4] *(replies to C1.4)* Ms Adeyemi acted reasonably in not depositing valuables since the burden was on the hotel to provide clear contractual terms and security prior to contract formation; the hotel cannot rely on a notice given post-contract to shift liability (J41:p3; F3, F6). — *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6

## Round 2: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C2.1** [I1] *(replies to A2.1)* The exclusion notice on the bedroom door was not incorporated into the contract because the contract was completed at the reception desk prior to seeing the notice, as in Olley where the bedroom notice was held not part of the contract for want of prior notice. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C2.2** [I2] *(replies to A2.2)* The hotel owed no broader duty beyond contractual terms for guests' possessions; unlike Donoghue's manufacturer-consumer context, the hotel's role is purely contractual with no positive duty to protect property, limiting duty of care claims. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
- **C2.3** [I3] *(replies to A2.3)* The exclusion clause is valid and enforceable because the hotel satisfied reasonable steps to give notice through the door notice present before use of the room, analogous to Parker where reasonable notice sufficed, and Photo Production confirms clear exclusion clauses take effect despite breaches. — *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶3; *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C2.4** [I4] *(replies to A2.4)* Ms Adeyemi failed reasonable care by not depositing valuables at reception as per the notice, and the hotel is entitled to rely on this to exclude liability, consistent with Parker where actual notice and guest compliance were key. — *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6
