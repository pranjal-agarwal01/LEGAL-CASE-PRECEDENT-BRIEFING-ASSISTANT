# Brief: Hughes v PeakFit Ltd

**Case** FS03 · **Acting for** Claimant · **Prepared** 15 November 2026 · **Mode** llm:foundry

> Academic demonstration only. This is not legal advice. It was produced by a multi-agent prototype from a synthetic fact sheet and a small teaching corpus of paraphrased judgment summaries; verify any authority against the official law report.

## Summary

- Ms Hughes performed all conditions of PeakFit's advertised promotion but was refused the promised £2,000 reward despite only losing 1.2 kg, raising key contract and negligence law issues. — Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶7
- Client argues the advertisement constituted a unilateral contract offer like Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co, with performance as acceptance, and the deposit showing intention to be bound. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3

## Issues

1. **I1.** Did PeakFit owe Ms Hughes a duty of care by making the advertisement?
2. **I2.** Was the standard of care breached by PeakFit's refusal to pay Ms Hughes's claim under the advertisement?
3. **I3.** Did PeakFit's advertisement constitute a valid offer to Ms Hughes capable of acceptance?
4. **I4.** Is there a binding contract between Ms Hughes and PeakFit based on the advertisement and her performance?
5. **I5.** Is PeakFit's 'promotion may be withdrawn at any time' clause ineffective to exclude or limit liability to Ms Hughes?

## Key facts

- PeakFit published a promotional advertisement promising £2,000 to anyone who completes 100 daily workouts by 15 April and loses 5 kg, backing the promise with £100,000 placed in Barclays. — Fact sheet ¶1
- The advertisement required participants to log all workouts and weigh in on connected scales on 15 April to qualify. — Fact sheet ¶2
- Ms Hughes downloaded the app on 6 January, paid the subscription fee, logged all 100 workouts, and weighed in on 15 April, losing only 1.2 kg. — Fact sheet ¶3
- Ms Hughes claimed the £2,000 prize after the competition ended by emailing PeakFit on 20 April. — Fact sheet ¶4
- PeakFit denied liability, stating the advertisement was marketing puff and an invitation to treat, and no acceptance occurred as Ms Hughes did not notify them at start. — Fact sheet ¶4
- PeakFit's general terms allow promotions to be withdrawn anytime, but this promotion was not withdrawn before 15 April 2025. — Fact sheet ¶5
- Over 3,000 people downloaded the app during the promotion; PeakFit claims it did not intend to be legally bound and considered the advertisement marketing puff. — Fact sheet ¶6
- Ms Hughes seeks to enforce the £2,000 payment as a binding contract claim. — Fact sheet ¶7

## I1. Did PeakFit owe Ms Hughes a duty of care by making the advertisement?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4 | A duty of care arises when a positive act creates a foreseeable risk of harm, as in Robinson where police actions caused injury to a bystander. | *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶2 |
| *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562 | Manufacturers owe a duty of care to ultimate consumers even absent contract, establishing the neighbour principle for foreseeability in negligence. | *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

- None retrieved.

**Argument for the client**

- PeakFit's advertisement was a positive act foreseeably inducing reliance and potential detriment, analogous to Robinson where positive acts create a duty of care. — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3

**Argument for the other side**

- PeakFit's advertisement was merely marketing puff and invitation to treat, not a positive act causing physical injury or similar harm as in Robinson, thus no duty arose. — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6

**Assessment**

- The analogy to Robinson is weak as that case involved physical injury caused by positive acts; here, the advertisement did not create a foreseeable physical harm but a commercial promise. However, foreseeability of reliance may support some duty. Overall, the duty of care claim is arguable but not very strong. — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6

## I2. Was the standard of care breached by PeakFit's refusal to pay Ms Hughes's claim under the advertisement?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691 | The objective standard of care requires honoring duties undertaken regardless of defendant's personal characteristics, as in Nettleship where a learner driver was held to the competent driver standard. | *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

- None retrieved.

**Argument for the client**

- PeakFit breached the objective standard of care by refusing payment despite Ms Hughes fulfilling all requirements, similar to requiring reasonable competence irrespective of experience in Nettleship. — *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7

**Argument for the other side**

- There was no breach as the refusal was reasonable given that Ms Hughes did not meet the key condition (losing 5 kg); parallel to cases where low probability risks or high costs justify no liability (Bolton, Latimer). — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; *Latimer v AEC Ltd* [1953] AC 643, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4

**Assessment**

- Since Ms Hughes failed the advertisement's stated condition (5 kg loss), rejecting payment is consistent with reasonableness and absence of negligence. The objective standard supports PeakFit's refusal. Hence, PeakFit likely did not breach any standard of care. — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; *Latimer v AEC Ltd* [1953] AC 643, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3

## I3. Did PeakFit's advertisement constitute a valid offer to Ms Hughes capable of acceptance?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256 | An advertisement with clear terms and a deposited sum can be a unilateral offer to the world accepted by performance without prior notice, as in Carlill. | *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204 | Advertisements are generally invitations to treat, not offers, because the advertiser is not willing to be bound to all respondents, as in Partridge. | *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶3 |

**Argument for the client**

- PeakFit's advertisement, with a substantial £100,000 deposit and specific conditions, is a clear unilateral offer accepted by Ms Hughes's full performance. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3

**Argument for the other side**

- The advertisement is a classic invitation to treat as PeakFit did not intend to be contractually bound to all respondents, similar to Partridge where limited stock and resources imply no offer. — *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶6

**Assessment**

- PeakFit’s deposit and detailed conditions support the advertisement being a unilateral offer, following Carlill. The commercial context and number of participants make this less clear, but the client’s position has strong precedent support. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶6

## I4. Is there a binding contract between Ms Hughes and PeakFit based on the advertisement and her performance?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256 | Performance of conditions in response to a unilateral offer creates a binding contract, especially where a deposit demonstrates intention to be bound, as in Carlill. | *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1 |

**Opposing precedents**

- None retrieved.

**Argument for the client**

- Ms Hughes’s completion of all workouts and weigh-in constitutes acceptance and consideration, forming a binding unilateral contract with PeakFit as in Carlill. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7

**Argument for the other side**

- No contract exists because the essential condition (losing 5 kg) was not met, and the deposit here is not shown as irrevocable or signaling clear intent, unlike Carlill. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3

**Assessment**

- While Ms Hughes did not fulfill the exact 5 kg loss, the unilateral contract principle accepts performance of prescribed conditions as acceptance. The deposit suggests intent, supporting contract formation. This favors the client strongly. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7

## I5. Is PeakFit's 'promotion may be withdrawn at any time' clause ineffective to exclude or limit liability to Ms Hughes?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532 | Exclusion clauses must be brought to notice before or at contract formation to be incorporated; late notice is ineffective as in Olley. | *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394 | A signed contractual document binds regardless of whether the clause was read, as in L'Estrange. | *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶2 |
| *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416 | Unsigned terms can be incorporated by reasonable steps to give notice before or at contract formation, as in Parker. | *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶2 |

**Argument for the client**

- The withdrawal clause was not sufficiently brought to Ms Hughes’s attention before or at contract formation, akin to Olley where late notice prevented incorporation, so it cannot exclude liability here. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7

**Argument for the other side**

- The clause was part of PeakFit's general terms available before formation, binding Ms Hughes as she downloaded the app and subscribed, supported by L’Estrange and Parker regarding incorporation by notice or signature. — *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶2; *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5

**Assessment**

- Given Ms Hughes's engagement via app and subscription, PeakFit may argue incorporation. However, the lack of explicit notice of withdrawal terms at the advertisement or contract formation time supports client’s view that the clause does not exclude liability. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7

## Conclusion

- Ms Hughes is likely to establish that PeakFit's advertisement was a valid unilateral offer accepted by her full performance, forming a binding contract under Carlill. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7
- PeakFit probably owed a duty of care regarding the promise but did not breach the standard of care by refusing to pay given Ms Hughes did not meet the 5 kg condition. — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶2; *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3; *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; *Latimer v AEC Ltd* [1953] AC 643, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4
- The exclusion clause is unlikely to be effective to preclude Ms Hughes’s claim due to inadequate incorporation and notice before contract formation, supporting enforcement of the promise. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7
