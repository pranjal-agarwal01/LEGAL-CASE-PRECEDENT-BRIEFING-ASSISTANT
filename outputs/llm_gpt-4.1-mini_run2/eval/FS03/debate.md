# Debate transcript: Hughes v PeakFit Ltd

Mode: llm:foundry · LLM calls: 9

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] PeakFit owed Ms Hughes a duty of care in making the advertisement because they made a positive act creating a foreseeable risk that participants would rely on the advertisement’s promise and act to their detriment, analogous to the police's duty in Robinson v Chief Constable where positive acts gave rise to duty (J19:p2). — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A1.2** [I2] PeakFit breached the standard of care by refusing to pay Ms Hughes, who performed all conditions but only lost 1.2 kg, because the objective standard is that a party making a unilateral contract must honour clear promises, similar to the objective standards imposed in Nettleship v Weston despite inexperience (J11:p3; F3, F7). — *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7
- **A1.3** [I3] The advertisement was a valid offer, as shown in Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co where an advertisement depositing money with a bank to show sincerity was held a unilateral offer to the world, accepted by performing conditions; here PeakFit's £100,000 deposit similarly shows intention and definiteness (J23:p1, J23:p3; F1, F3). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A1.4** [I4] A binding contract exists between Ms Hughes and PeakFit as in Carlill, where performance of the conditions is acceptance and consideration, and the deposit of funds demonstrates an intention to be legally bound; thus, Ms Hughes's completion of all workouts and weigh-in establishes contract formation (J23:p2; F1, F3, F7). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7
- **A1.5** [I5] PeakFit's clause that promotions 'may be withdrawn at any time' does not exclude or limit liability here because the promotion was not withdrawn before 15 April 2025 when Ms Hughes completed the conditions, and the clause was not notified in a manner sufficient to incorporate it into the contract, like the late notice in Olley v Marlborough Court (J42:p2, J42:p3; F5, F7). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] *(replies to A1.1)* PeakFit's advertisement was a marketing promotion and invitation to treat, not a positive act creating a duty of care akin to the police's duty in Robinson, which involved physical injury from positive acts, whereas here there was no such harm or duty (J19:p3; F4, F6). — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C1.2** [I2] *(replies to A1.2)* There is no breach of standard of care because PeakFit's refusal to pay is not negligence; liability is limited by the reasonable scope of the advertisement as a marketing puff, and no objectively reasonable standard requires payment when conditions for the £2,000 were not met (J12:p2, J14:p2; F3, F4). — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; *Latimer v AEC Ltd* [1953] AC 643, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4
- **C1.3** [I3] *(replies to A1.3)* The advertisement was an invitation to treat not an offer, like in Partridge v Crittenden where advertisements were held not to be offers due to limited stock and inability to be bound to all (J24:p2, J24:p3; F1, F6). The sincerity deposit here does not suffice to convert it to an offer. — *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C1.4** [I4] *(replies to A1.4)* No binding contract existed; unlike Carlill, the deposit here was not shown to be irrevocable or indicative of contract intent, and Ms Hughes's failure to meet the promise's condition of losing 5 kg means no acceptance by performance (J23:p3; F3). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C1.5** [I5] *(replies to A1.5)* The clause 'promotion may be withdrawn at any time' effectively limits liability; since it was incorporated in PeakFit's general terms and was available before and during the promotion, it binds participants unlike in Olley as it was provided prior to contract formation (J40:p2, J41:p2; F5). — *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶2; *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* PeakFit's advertisement created a foreseeable risk that participants would rely on the promise to their detriment, establishing a duty of care by their positive act, analogous to Robinson where positive acts created foreseeable risk and duty (J19:p2; F1, F3). — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A2.2** [I2] *(replies to C1.2)* PeakFit breached the standard of care by refusing to pay Ms Hughes who fulfilled all promotional conditions; the objective standard requires honoring the clear promise, just as Nettleship imposed a standard despite the defendant's inexperience (J11:p3; F3, F7). — *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7
- **A2.3** [I3] *(replies to C1.3)* The advertisement was a valid unilateral offer, akin to Carlill where a deposited sum showed intent, and Ms Hughes accepted by performing all conditions without needing to notify PeakFit (J23:p1, J23:p3; F1, F3). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A2.4** [I4] *(replies to C1.4)* A binding contract existed as in Carlill since performance constituted acceptance and consideration, with the deposit showing clear intention to be bound, so Ms Hughes's compliance establishes contract formation despite partial weight loss (J23:p2; F1, F3, F7). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7
- **A2.5** [I5] *(replies to C1.5)* The withdrawal clause was not incorporated into the contract as it was not sufficiently brought to Ms Hughes's attention before or at contract formation, analogous to Olley where late notification failed incorporation (J42:p2, J42:p3; F5, F7). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7

## Round 2: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C2.1** [I1] *(replies to A2.1)* PeakFit's advertisement was a marketing promotion and invitation to treat, not a positive act creating a duty of care akin to that in Robinson where physical injury was caused through police positive acts, absent here (J19:p3; F4, F6). — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C2.2** [I2] *(replies to A2.2)* Refusing to pay £2,000 does not breach any standard of care as it is not negligence; the circumstances show it was reasonable refusal given that Ms Hughes did not meet the 5 kg condition, akin to the standard in Bolton v Stone and Latimer where risk probability and cost of precautions were weighed (J12:p2; J14:p2; F3, F4). — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; *Latimer v AEC Ltd* [1953] AC 643, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4
- **C2.3** [I3] *(replies to A2.3)* The advertisement was an invitation to treat not an offer, following Partridge where advertisements cannot bind the advertiser to all respondents due to limited resources; the deposited sum here does not suffice to convert it into a contractual offer (J24:p2; F1, F6). — *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C2.4** [I4] *(replies to A2.4)* No binding contract arose; unlike Carlill, the deposit here was not shown to be irrevocably deposited nor indicative of clear intention to be bound, and Ms Hughes failing to lose the promised 5 kg means she did not perform the condition of acceptance (J23:p3; F3). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C2.5** [I5] *(replies to A2.5)* The clause 'promotion may be withdrawn at any time' was incorporated before or during the contract, binding Ms Hughes; unlike Olley where notice was after contract formation, here the clause was available from the start, supported by L'Estrange and Parker which uphold terms incorporated by prior notice or signature (J40:p2; J41:p2; F5). — *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶2; *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
