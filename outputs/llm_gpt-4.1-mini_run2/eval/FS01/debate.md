# Debate transcript: Nair v Brightwater Beverages Ltd

Mode: llm:foundry · LLM calls: 9

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] Brightwater owed a duty of care to Ms Nair as a consumer because it manufactured a product intended for consumption by end users like her, who could not inspect the contents due to the opaque sealed bottle; under the neighbour principle, the manufacturer should have foreseen harm to such consumers and owed them reasonable care, even absent a contract, as established in Donoghue v Stevenson. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶1; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶8
- **A1.2** [I2] Brightwater breached the standard of care in bottling the kombucha because the presence of glass and mould shows a failure to meet the objective standard of a reasonably competent manufacturer taking precautions to ensure safety; the duty is to avoid reasonably foreseeable harm to consumers, analogous to the standard in Donoghue v Stevenson and Nettleship v Weston for objective negligence assessment. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶1; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A1.3** [I3] Ms Nair's pre-existing gastric ulcer is relevant only to the extent it increased the severity of her injuries, but does not negate causation, as per the eggshell skull rule in Smith v Leech Brain where the defendant must take the claimant as found, including vulnerabilities; the injury was a foreseeable type of harm, satisfying causation. — *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A1.4** [I4] Ms Nair's losses are recoverable despite lack of contract with Brightwater because negligence protects physical consumers from harm caused by defective products, as held in Donoghue v Stevenson; loss consequential on physical injury is not pure economic loss and thus recoverable, unlike pure financial loss liabilities limited in Caparo. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3; *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶8

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] *(replies to A1.1)* Brightwater did not owe Ms Nair a duty of care because the duty requires proximity and it must be fair, just, and reasonable to impose such a duty. The case law requires incremental development of negligence categories by analogy with established categories, and here no such established relationship exists as per Caparo Industries plc v Dickman [J02:p3]. — *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3
- **C1.2** [I2] *(replies to A1.2)* Brightwater did not breach the standard of care as the presence of glass and mould may not have been reasonably foreseeable or frequent enough to require additional measures beyond those taken. The law requires weighing the cost and practicability of precautions against the size of the risk, and a risk may be so small it does not warrant further precautions, as in Bolton v Stone [J12:p2] and Latimer v AEC Ltd [J14:p3]. — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; *Latimer v AEC Ltd* [1953] AC 643, ¶3
- **C1.3** [I3] *(replies to A1.3)* Ms Nair has not proven causation on the balance of probabilities because the ulcer and other factors may have contributed to the injuries; mere addition of another possible cause does not suffice, per Wilsher v Essex Area Health Authority [J22:p3] and the but-for test requires proof that the breach caused the harm, as emphasised in Barnett v Chelsea and Kensington Hospital Management Committee [J08:p3]. — *Wilsher v Essex Area Health Authority* [1988] AC 1074, ¶3; *Barnett v Chelsea and Kensington Hospital Management Committee* [1969] 1 QB 428, ¶3
- **C1.4** [I4] *(replies to A1.4)* Ms Nair's losses are not recoverable as pure economic loss without contract is generally not recoverable and negligence claims for recovery of economic loss remain limited; imposing such liability here would be neither fair nor just, reiterating the restrictive approach in Caparo Industries plc v Dickman [J02:p3]. — *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* Brightwater owed Ms Nair a duty of care as a consumer of its product because, like in Donoghue v Stevenson, the product was sold in a sealed bottle intended to reach the ultimate consumer without intermediate inspection, and harm from defective products was reasonably foreseeable. The absence of contract between Ms Nair and Brightwater is no bar to this duty under the neighbour principle. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A2.2** [I2] *(replies to C1.2)* Brightwater breached the standard of care expected of a reasonably competent manufacturer by allowing a sharp sliver of glass and mould in a sealed bottle, indicating a failure to take reasonable precautions in manufacturing and bottling. This objective standard is supported by Donoghue v Stevenson and Nettleship v Weston which focus on avoiding reasonably foreseeable harm to consumers. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶1; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2
- **A2.3** [I3] *(replies to C1.3)* Ms Nair's pre-existing gastric ulcer is relevant only to the severity of the injury; the type of injury (laceration caused by glass) was foreseeable and caused by Brightwater's breach, so causation is satisfied under the eggshell skull rule from Smith v Leech Brain which requires taking the claimant as found. — *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A2.4** [I4] *(replies to C1.4)* Ms Nair's losses resulting from physical injury are recoverable despite no contract with Brightwater because they arise from physical harm to the consumer caused by a defective product, consistent with Donoghue v Stevenson. These losses are not pure economic loss and recoverable as consequential damage from negligence. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6; Fact sheet ¶8

## Round 2: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C2.1** [I1] *(replies to A2.1)* The claimant does not enjoy a duty of care from Brightwater because, per Caparo, in novel situations a duty requires foreseeability, proximity, and that imposing a duty be fair, just, and reasonable; here, no special relationship or proximity akin to established categories exists and incremental development is required, so imposing a duty is inappropriate. — *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3
- **C2.2** [I2] *(replies to A2.2)* Even assuming the risk of glass or mould contamination existed, it was so unlikely that a reasonable manufacturer would not be expected to take extraordinary precautions beyond a passed safety inspection, as the risk is minimal and disproportionate measures are not required (Latimer, Bolton). — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; *Latimer v AEC Ltd* [1953] AC 643, ¶3
- **C2.3** [I3] *(replies to A2.3)* Causation fails because the claimant cannot prove on the balance of probabilities that Brightwater's breach caused the injury; as in Wilsher, introducing an additional possible cause amid several (here, claimant's ulcer and possible cafe tampering) does not suffice, and Barnett confirms that a breach which does not affect the outcome is no basis for liability. — *Wilsher v Essex Area Health Authority* [1988] AC 1074, ¶3; *Barnett v Chelsea and Kensington Hospital Management Committee* [1969] 1 QB 428, ¶3
- **C2.4** [I4] *(replies to A2.4)* Ms Nair's losses are not recoverable in negligence absent a contract since these amount to pure economic loss, which Caparo restricts unless a special relationship exists; consequently, damages for lost earnings and medical expenses are not recoverable here. — *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3; *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶3
