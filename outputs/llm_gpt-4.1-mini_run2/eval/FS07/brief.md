# Brief: Brennan Joinery Ltd v Castlegate Developments Ltd

## Summary

- Brennan Joinery Ltd seeks to enforce Castlegate Developments Ltd's promise to pay an additional £18,000 for timely completion of remaining houses under a variation or promissory estoppel. — Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7
- Williams v Roffey Bros supports the validity of a contract variation based on practical benefit obtained by the promisor, distinguishing the old rule in Stilk v Myrick. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶2; *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3
- The High Trees case establishes promissory estoppel empowering enforcement of promises intended to be binding and acted upon, even absent consideration. — *Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd* [1947] KB 130, ¶3

## Issues

1. **I1.** Is there a valid variation to the contract between Brennan Joinery Ltd and Castlegate Developments Ltd obliging Castlegate to pay the extra £18,000?
2. **I2.** Can Brennan Joinery Ltd enforce Castlegate Developments Ltd's promise to pay the extra £18,000 on the basis of promissory estoppel?

## Key facts

- Brennan Joinery Ltd was contracted by Castlegate to fit kitchens and staircases in 30 new townhouses for £150,000, to be completed by June 30, 2025 — Fact sheet ¶1
- Castlegate's contract with the developer contains a liquidated damages clause of £5,000 per day for late completion — Fact sheet ¶2
- By April 2025 Brennan had completed 12 houses and indicated financial difficulties and possible failure to finish the job — Fact sheet ¶3
- On 14 April 2025 Castlegate promised to pay Brennan an extra £1,000 per remaining house completed on time to avoid penalties — Fact sheet ¶4
- Brennan completed the remaining 18 houses by the deadline, but Castlegate only paid the original contract price and refused the extra £18,000 — Fact sheet ¶5
- There was no threat or pressure from Brennan to obtain Castlegate’s promise; the offer of extra payment came solely from Castlegate — Fact sheet ¶6
- Brennan seeks to recover the £18,000 promised in extra payment — Fact sheet ¶7

## I1. Is there a valid variation to the contract between Brennan Joinery Ltd and Castlegate Developments Ltd obliging Castlegate to pay the extra £18,000?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1 | Williams v Roffey Bros establishes that a promise of extra payment is enforceable if it confers a practical benefit on the promisor without economic duress or fraud. | *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶2; *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317 | Stilk v Myrick holds that performance of an existing contractual duty is not good consideration for a promise to pay extra, rendering such promises unenforceable. | *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶2; *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3 |

**Argument for the client**

- The promise by Castlegate to pay an extra £1,000 for each remaining house completed on time constitutes a valid contract variation because Castlegate obtained a practical benefit: avoiding the developer's penalty and the trouble of finding another joiner. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6
- The practical benefit rule from Williams v Roffey Bros applies as there was no economic duress or fraud, and Brennan's timely completion gave Castlegate clear advantages. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶2; *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6

**Argument for the other side**

- The promise of extra payment is unenforceable for lack of consideration since Brennan was contractually obliged to complete all 30 houses by the deadline; performing this existing duty cannot provide fresh consideration, as held in Stilk v Myrick. — *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶2; *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶5
- Williams v Roffey Bros only refines Stilk v Myrick and requires some indication of refusal to perform or alteration of obligation, neither of which is present here, distinguishing the case facts. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6

**Assessment**

- The balance of authorities favors finding a valid variation under Williams v Roffey Bros since Castlegate obtained a practical benefit preventing penalties and replacement costs, and there was no economic duress; the absence of a refusal to perform does not preclude variation when a practical benefit is gained. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶2; *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6

## I2. Can Brennan Joinery Ltd enforce Castlegate Developments Ltd's promise to pay the extra £18,000 on the basis of promissory estoppel?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd* [1947] KB 130 | High Trees establishes that a promise intended to be binding and acted upon can be enforced under promissory estoppel, even without consideration. | *Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd* [1947] KB 130, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

- None retrieved.

**Argument for the client**

- Castlegate's promise was intended to be binding and was acted upon by Brennan completing the houses on schedule, creating a promissory estoppel preventing Castlegate from denying the payment obligation. — *Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd* [1947] KB 130, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- Even if consideration is absent, promissory estoppel applies to prevent injustice where a party relies on a clear promise made to them. — *Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd* [1947] KB 130, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5

**Argument for the other side**

- Promissory estoppel cannot create a new cause of action or an independent basis to enforce Castlegate's promise where no fresh consideration exists; it only suspends rights under existing contracts as per High Trees. — *Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd* [1947] KB 130, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5

**Assessment**

- Although promissory estoppel prevents Castlegate from denying the promise, it traditionally does not provide an independent cause of action to enforce new payment obligations absent consideration, limiting its practical application here. — *Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd* [1947] KB 130, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5

## Conclusion

- Brennan Joinery Ltd has a strong argument that Castlegate's promise is enforceable as a valid contract variation based on practical benefit under Williams v Roffey Bros, which fits the facts of avoiding penalties and additional costs without duress. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶2; *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6
- Promissory estoppel offers a secondary but less robust basis for enforcement, as it precludes Castlegate from reneging on its promise but traditionally does not independently create enforceable new payment obligations without consideration. — *Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd* [1947] KB 130, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- Overall, Brennan's best position lies in showing the practical benefit conferred to justify the contract variation; reliance on promissory estoppel provides supplementary support primarily aimed at preventing unconscionable denial of the promise. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; *Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd* [1947] KB 130, ¶3
