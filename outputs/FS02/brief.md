## Summary

- Acting for Daniel Okafor (claimant), this brief addresses 3 issue(s) arising from Okafor v St Aldhelm's Hospital NHS Trust: Mr Okafor wants to claim damages in negligence for the failure to warn him of a material risk and of the alternative treatment before he consented to surgery. — Fact sheet ¶8
- Did St Aldhelm's Hospital NHS Trust breach the duty to warn Daniel Okafor of material risks and reasonable alternatives before consent was given? Favourable for Daniel Okafor. — *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶3; *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* [1957] 1 WLR 582, ¶3
- Did the clinician's conduct fall below the standard of a responsible body of medical opinion? Finely balanced for Daniel Okafor. — *Bolitho v City and Hackney Health Authority* [1998] AC 232, ¶3
- Can Daniel Okafor prove that the breach caused the harm, on the but-for test or a modified approach? Unfavourable for Daniel Okafor. — *Barnett v Chelsea and Kensington Hospital Management Committee* [1969] 1 QB 428, ¶3

## Issues

1. **I1.** Did St Aldhelm's Hospital NHS Trust breach the duty to warn Daniel Okafor of material risks and reasonable alternatives before consent was given?
2. **I2.** Did the clinician's conduct fall below the standard of a responsible body of medical opinion?
3. **I3.** Can Daniel Okafor prove that the breach caused the harm, on the but-for test or a modified approach?

## Key facts

- On 2 February 2024 Mr Okafor consulted Mr Lyle about chronic neck pain radiating into his left arm. — Fact sheet ¶1
- Mr Lyle recommended cervical decompression surgery. He did not mention the roughly 2 per cent risk of damage to the nerve root supplying the hand, and he did not mention the alternative of a further six months of physiotherapy and steroid injections. — Fact sheet ¶2
- During the consultation Mr Okafor told Mr Lyle that his career depended on fine finger control of his left hand. — Fact sheet ¶3
- Mr Okafor signed a consent form and the operation took place on 11 April 2024. It was performed with proper skill. — Fact sheet ¶4
- The risk of nerve damage materialised. Mr Okafor has permanently lost fine motor control in two fingers of his left hand and can no longer perform professionally. — Fact sheet ¶5
- Mr Okafor says that if he had been warned of the risk he would not have consented to surgery on that date but would have tried physiotherapy first and sought a second opinion. He accepts he might eventually have had the same operation. — Fact sheet ¶6
- The Trust relies on evidence from two surgeons that many responsible spinal surgeons in 2024 did not routinely mention a 2 per cent risk to patients, and argues that the surgery itself was not negligent so the failure to warn caused no loss. — Fact sheet ¶7
