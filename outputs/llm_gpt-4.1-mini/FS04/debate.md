# Debate transcript: Rowe v Skyline Terraces Ltd

Mode: llm:foundry · LLM calls: 9

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] Skyline's promise in its online listing that the terrace offers "front-row seats for the display" of the fireworks, combined with Mr Rowe's emails stating his purpose was to watch the fireworks, forms a clear offer that was accepted by payment of the deposit, demonstrating intention to be bound as in Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co [J23:p2] applied to facts [F2], [F1]. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2
- **A1.2** [I2] The contract was frustrated by the cancellation of the fireworks because the event was the foundation of the contract recognised by both parties, and its cancellation defeated the common purpose of Mr Rowe's terrace hire, even though the terrace was still physically available, per Krell v Henry [J37:p3] applied to facts [F4],[F2],[F5]. — *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] The online listing advertising the terrace as offering 'the best view' of the fireworks is analogous to an invitation to treat, not a binding contractual promise about the fireworks event itself. Advertisements are generally not offers, as established by Partridge v Crittenden and Fisher v Bell, which hold advertisements are invitations to treat, not offers for contract (J24:p2; J50:p3). — *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶3
- **C1.2** [I1] Given the written hire agreement only includes 'exclusive use of the terrace, 6pm to midnight, with bar service' and is silent on the fireworks, the contract terms exclude the fireworks event as a binding contractual term, consistent with the principle that terms not brought to the notice at or before contract formation cannot be incorporated (Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd) (J42:p3; F3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C1.3** [I2] Frustration doctrine requires that the supervening event make the contractual performance radically different or impossible, not merely less profitable or frustrating one party's commercial purpose (Davis Contractors Ltd v Fareham Urban District Council) (J39:p3; F4,F6). Here, the terrace was still available and usable despite the fireworks cancellation (F5), so the cancellation did not render the contract radically different. — *Davis Contractors Ltd v Fareham Urban District Council* [1956] AC 696, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- **C1.4** [I2] In Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton, cancellation of a naval review did not frustrate a charter where other uses remained possible; similarly, Mr Rowe's event remained physically possible and was merely less attractive without the fireworks (F5,F6), so frustration does not apply (J38:p3). — *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* The advertisement by Skyline promising 'front-row seats for the display' alongside Mr Rowe's emails expressing his purpose to watch the fireworks, constitutes a clear offer which was accepted by Mr Rowe's payment of the deposit, showing intention to be bound, consistent with Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co where an advertisement was held to be an offer and not a mere invitation to treat, supported by the deposit showing intent to be bound (J23:p2; F2, F1). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1
- **A2.2** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* The general principle that advertisements are invitations to treat, as in Partridge v Crittenden and Fisher v Bell, does not apply here because Skyline's promise was not a mere price or invitation, but a clear promise of front-row seats for the fireworks display, supported by Mr Rowe's communications about the purpose, creating a binding assurance beyond a mere invitation to treat (J23:p2, contrasting J24:p2, J50:p3; F2). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2
- **A2.3** [I1] *(replies to C1.2)* Although the written hire agreement does not mention the fireworks, the parties' prior communications and the online listing formed part of the contract by express or implied incorporation since they established a fundamental basis and representation on which Mr Rowe entered the contract, and Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd does not disapply incorporation where prior representations clearly induce contract formation (contrasting J42:p3; F2, F3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A2.4** [I2] *(replies to C1.3)* The cancellation of the fireworks frustrated the contract because the fireworks formed the foundation of the contract as jointly understood by the parties, and their cancellation destroyed the common purpose even though literal performance (i.e., terrace hire) was still possible, paralleling the principle in Krell v Henry that frustration arises when a supervening event destroys a state of affairs fundamental to the contract (J37:p3; F4, F2, F5). — *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A2.5** [I2] *(replies to C1.3)* Unlike in Davis Contractors and Herne Bay, where the event frustrated was only one party's commercial purpose and alternate uses remained, here both parties recognised the fireworks as the basis of the contract, so frustration applies because the purpose was common, not unilateral, thus the risk lies not with Mr Rowe (contrasting J39:p3, J38:p3; F4, F2, F5, F6). — *Davis Contractors Ltd v Fareham Urban District Council* [1956] AC 696, ¶3; *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6
- **A2.6** [I2] *(replies to C1.4)* The terrace, bar and staff remaining available after cancellation does not defeat frustration because the foundation of the contract, the fireworks viewing, was destroyed, applying Krell v Henry where literal performance was possible but frustration still arose (J37:p3; F5). — *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 2: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C2.1** [I1] *(replies to A2.1)* The advertisement in the online listing by Skyline was not a binding offer but an invitation to treat, as established in Partridge v Crittenden where advertisements were held not to be offers but invitations and in Fisher v Bell where display of goods with price tags was an invitation to treat; here the listing's advertisement resembles an invitation to treat rather than a definite contractual offer. — *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶3
- **C2.2** [I1] *(replies to A2.3)* The written hire agreement's terms, specifying exclusive use of the terrace with bar service and without reference to the fireworks, exclude incorporation of any prior representations or advertisements about the fireworks, in line with Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd holding that terms must be brought to notice at or before contract formation to be incorporated; the fireworks were not a contractual term here. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C2.3** [I2] *(replies to A2.4)* The cancellation of the fireworks does not frustrate the contract as the hire of the terrace was still physically possible, and the performance was not radically different from what was agreed; in Davis Contractors hardship or diminished attraction is insufficient for frustration, and here the terrace, bar, and staff remained available. — *Davis Contractors Ltd v Fareham Urban District Council* [1956] AC 696, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C2.4** [I2] *(replies to A2.5)* This situation aligns with Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton, where cancellation of the naval review did not frustrate a charter because alternate uses remained available and the frustrated party assumed the risk of the venture falling short; similarly, Mr Rowe's purpose to watch fireworks does not create frustration as the terrace hire was still viable. — *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C2.5** [I2] *(replies to A2.6)* Literal performance remains possible, and the supervening event must be such to make the contract radically different; the terrace remained available for use, thus the cancellation of the fireworks does not qualify as frustration under Krell v Henry's and the opposing cases' principles, given the difference in facts here where parties did not mutually base the contract on the fireworks alone. — *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶3; *Davis Contractors Ltd v Fareham Urban District Council* [1956] AC 696, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
