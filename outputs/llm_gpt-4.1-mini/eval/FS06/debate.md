# Debate transcript: Adeyemi v Harbourside Hotel Ltd

Mode: llm:foundry · LLM calls: 9

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] The hotel owed a duty of care to Ms Adeyemi to take reasonable steps to safeguard her property, as shown in Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd where the hotel was held responsible for theft due to its negligence with the room key (J42:p1) and the fundamental principles of duty of care apply to such circumstances. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶1
- **A1.2** [I2] The exclusion notice on the bedroom door was not incorporated into the contract because the contract was concluded at the reception desk before Ms Adeyemi saw the notice, exactly as held in Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd (J42:p2, J42:p3). Thus the hotel's reliance on the notice late in the contract formation is ineffective. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3
- **A1.3** [I3] According to Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd, terms referred to after the contract is concluded are not incorporated, especially exclusion clauses which are onerous and require clear notice; here the notice on the door came after contract formation at reception and was not sufficiently clear (J43:p2, J43:p3). — *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶2; *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶3
- **A1.4** [I4] The hotel did not give Ms Adeyemi adequate opportunity to know and accept the exclusion clause before contract formation as the clause was only on a notice inside the room; per Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd terms must be communicated before or at the time of contract (J42:p3), which did not happen here. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] *(replies to A1.1)* The hotel's duty of care should be evaluated under established negligence principles, but as this is not a novel or recognized category of liability for theft within a hotel room, and the police found no forced entry and a staff member left the key unattended (F3-F5), the hotel's direct liability for theft is not clearly established; the case Olley concerned incorporation, not duty of care per se (J42:p1). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶1
- **C1.2** [I2] *(replies to A1.2)* The exclusion notice, although on the bedroom door, was not incorporated because the contract was concluded at reception upon payment and key receipt (F2-F3), directly aligning with Olley where notice after contract conclusion was ineffective (J42:p2, J42:p3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3
- **C1.3** [I3] *(replies to A1.3)* Exclusion clauses must be incorporated before or at contract formation and clear to be effective; since the notice was only visible on the bedroom door after the contract concluded (F3-F4), and Ms Adeyemi had no prior exposure on the website (F4), the hotel cannot rely on the clause per Thornton which requires clear notice especially for onerous terms (J43:p2, J43:p3). — *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶2; *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶3
- **C1.4** [I4] *(replies to A1.4)* Adequate opportunity means communication of terms before or at contract conclusion; in this case, as in Olley, the clause was only seen after contract formation in the room (F2-F3), so the hotel failed to provide reasonable notice and opportunity to accept (J42:p3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] The hotel owed a duty of care to Ms Adeyemi to take reasonable care to safeguard her property, as shown in Olley where the hotel was held liable for theft due to negligence with the room key, thus under established negligence principles the hotel here is liable for the theft which occurred due to staff negligence (J42:p1; F3-F5). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A2.2** [I2] The exclusion clause on the bedroom door was not incorporated into the contract because, as in Olley, the contract was concluded at reception before the guest saw the notice; terms must be brought to notice before or at the time of contracting, and here the notice was only seen later in the room, so the clause is not part of the contract (J42:p2, J42:p3; F2, F3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A2.3** [I3] Exclusion clauses must be incorporated before or at contract formation and must be clearly communicated, especially if onerous; per Thornton, terms referred to post-formation are not incorporated and onerous clauses require clear notice, which did not occur here with the notice on the bedroom door (J43:p2, J43:p3; F2, F3). — *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶2; *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A2.4** [I4] Ms Adeyemi was not given adequate opportunity to know or accept the exclusion clause before the contract concluded, as a clause seen after contract formation cannot bind; Olley confirms that terms must be communicated prior to or at contract formation, which here did not happen (J42:p3; F2, F3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3

## Round 2: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C2.1** [I1] *(replies to A2.1)* The hotel owes no duty of care to Ms Adeyemi for the theft since liability for property loss in hotel rooms hinges on contractual terms, not negligence, as Olley involved exclusion clause incorporation, not duty of care per se, and does not impose negligence liability for staff's failure in key security (J42:p1; F3-F5). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
- **C2.2** [I2] *(replies to A2.2)* The exclusion clause notice on the bedroom door was not incorporated into the contract as it was presented after the contract was concluded at reception upon payment and key receipt, mirroring the facts and legal conclusion in Olley where a bedroom notice was ineffective to vary contractual terms made at reception (J42:p2, J42:p3; F2, F3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C2.3** [I3] *(replies to A2.3)* The exclusion clause is ineffective because it was not incorporated before or at the time of contract formation and was only visible after contract conclusion, which is insufficient especially for onerous terms, as per Thornton where clauses referred to after contract formation are not incorporated and require clear notice (J43:p2, J43:p3; F2, F3). — *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶2; *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C2.4** [I4] *(replies to A2.4)* Ms Adeyemi was not given an adequate opportunity to know or accept the exclusion clause before the contract was concluded because, consistent with Olley, terms coming to notice only after the contract falls outside incorporation requirements (J42:p3; F2, F3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
