# Brief: Adeyemi v Harbourside Hotel Ltd

**Case** FS06 · **Acting for** Claimant · **Prepared** 15 November 2026 · **Mode** llm:foundry

> Academic demonstration only. This is not legal advice. It was produced by a multi-agent prototype from a synthetic fact sheet and a small teaching corpus of paraphrased judgment summaries; verify any authority against the official law report.

## Summary

- Ms Adeyemi checked into the hotel without a prior booking and signed the registration card with no reference to the exclusion clause, which appeared only on a notice inside the room after contract formation (F1-F3). — Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- The hotel failed to take reasonable care to protect her property, as the theft occurred due to staff negligence in leaving a master key unattended (F5). — Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
- The exclusion clause was not incorporated into the contract because it was brought to Ms Adeyemi's attention only after the contract was concluded at reception, following the reasoning in Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd (J42:p2, J42:p3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3
- Onerous exclusion clauses such as this require clear notice before or at contract formation, which was not provided, per Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd (J43:p2, J43:p3). — *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶2; *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶3

## Issues

1. **I1.** Is the hotel liable for the theft of Ms Adeyemi's property despite the exclusion notice on the bedroom door?
2. **I2.** Is the exclusion notice on the bedroom door incorporated into the contract between Ms Adeyemi and the hotel?
3. **I3.** Is the exclusion clause effective to exclude the hotel's liability for loss or damage to guest property?
4. **I4.** Was Ms Adeyemi given adequate opportunity to know and accept the exclusion clause before the contract was concluded?

## Key facts

- Ms Adeyemi arrived at the Harbourside Hotel without a booking and requested a room on 12 August 2025. — Fact sheet ¶1
- She signed a registration card recording her name, address and car registration; the card contained no terms and conditions and no reference to any terms or exclusions. — Fact sheet ¶2
- A notice on the back of her bedroom door stated that the hotel accepted no liability for loss of or damage to guests' property and advised depositing valuables at reception. — Fact sheet ¶3
- The same exclusion terms appear on the hotel website, which Ms Adeyemi had never visited. — Fact sheet ¶4
- On 13 August 2025, her laptop and a diamond bracelet worth £6,500 in total were stolen from her room; police found no forced entry but a staff member had left a master key unattended. — Fact sheet ¶5
- The hotel relies on the bedroom door notice to exclude liability and claims Ms Adeyemi should have deposited her valuables at reception. — Fact sheet ¶6
- Ms Adeyemi wishes to recover the value of her stolen property and argues that the exclusion notice was not part of her contract with the hotel. — Fact sheet ¶7

## I1. Is the hotel liable for the theft of Ms Adeyemi's property despite the exclusion notice on the bedroom door?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532 | The hotel owed a duty of care to safeguard the guest's property and can be liable for negligence leading to theft. | *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶1 |

**Opposing precedents**

- None retrieved.

**Argument for the client**

- The hotel's negligence in leaving a master key unattended led to the theft of Ms Adeyemi's property, as in Olley where the hotel was held liable for theft due to key mishandling (J42:p1, F3-F5). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

**Argument for the other side**

- Liability for theft in hotel rooms typically depends on contractual terms rather than negligence, and Olley focused primarily on exclusion clause incorporation, not imposing a general duty of care for theft by staff negligence. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

**Assessment**

- The hotel's negligence in this case is striking and analogous to Olley, supporting liability for the theft. The absence of forced entry and the admitted staff fault strengthen the claim that a duty of care was breached, despite some argument that liability rests in contract terms. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

## I2. Is the exclusion notice on the bedroom door incorporated into the contract between Ms Adeyemi and the hotel?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532 | Exclusion clauses found on notices seen after contract formation are not incorporated into the contract. | *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

- None retrieved.

**Argument for the client**

- The contract was concluded at reception before Ms Adeyemi saw the exclusion notice in her room, so the clause is not part of the contract per Olley's holding (J42:p2, J42:p3; F2, F3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3

**Argument for the other side**

- The hotel argues the notice on the bedroom door is known to guests as a standard term, but this was only visible after contract conclusion and Ms Adeyemi had not seen it earlier on the website; hence, it was not incorporated. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3

**Assessment**

- Consistent with Olley, the exclusion clause was not incorporated because it appeared only after contract conclusion at reception. The lack of reference to such terms on the registration card and absence of prior notice on the website support non-incorporation. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3

## I3. Is the exclusion clause effective to exclude the hotel's liability for loss or damage to guest property?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163 | Exclusion clauses must be clearly brought to the attention of the party before or at contract formation to be effective, especially if onerous. | *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶2; *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

- None retrieved.

**Argument for the client**

- The notice on the bedroom door came after the contract was made at reception and thus cannot exclude liability, since onerous terms require clear and timely notice, as established in Thornton (J43:p2, J43:p3; F2, F3). — *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶2; *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3

**Argument for the other side**

- The hotel contends that displaying the exclusion on the bedroom door and on the website suffices, but absence of any prior communication makes the exclusion clause ineffective, especially given the stringent requirements for onerous exclusions. — *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶2; *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3

**Assessment**

- The exclusion clause is ineffective because it was not communicated before or at the time of contracting, which is a prerequisite for incorporation and effectiveness, particularly for clauses excluding liability for negligence or loss. — *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶2; *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3

## I4. Was Ms Adeyemi given adequate opportunity to know and accept the exclusion clause before the contract was concluded?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532 | Terms must be brought to a party's notice before or at contract formation to be binding. | *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

- None retrieved.

**Argument for the client**

- Ms Adeyemi was not shown the exclusion clause before or at the time of contracting at reception, so she had no reasonable opportunity to know of or accept it, per Olley (J42:p3; F2, F3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3

**Argument for the other side**

- The hotel argues the posted notice gave adequate notice, but such unilateral post-contract notices do not suffice for incorporation or acceptance of terms. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3

**Assessment**

- Given the contract was concluded before the guest saw the clause, the hotel failed to provide reasonable notice and opportunity to accept the exclusion clause, so it cannot bind Ms Adeyemi. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3

## Conclusion

- The hotel is liable for the theft because it owed Ms Adeyemi a duty of care which was breached by staff negligence in key management (J42:p1; F3-F5). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
- The exclusion clause on the bedroom door was not incorporated into the contract as it was communicated only after contract formation, consistent with Olley (J42:p2, J42:p3; F2, F3). — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- Additionally, the notice was not effective to exclude liability because onerous clauses require clear notice before or at contract formation, which was absent here (J43:p2, J43:p3; F2, F3). — *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶2; *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd* [1971] 2 QB 163, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
