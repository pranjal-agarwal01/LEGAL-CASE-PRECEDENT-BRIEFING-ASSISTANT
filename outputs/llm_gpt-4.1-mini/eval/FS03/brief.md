# Brief: Hughes v PeakFit Ltd

**Case** FS03 · **Acting for** Claimant · **Prepared** 15 November 2026 · **Mode** llm:foundry

## Summary

- Ms Hughes seeks to enforce a £2,000 payment promised by PeakFit in an online fitness challenge advertising campaign, based on her full performance under its terms by 15 April 2025. — Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7
- The claimant relies principally on the leading precedent Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co for establishing unilateral offer, intention to create legal relations demonstrated by deposit of £100,000, and contract formation by complete performance without notification of acceptance. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3
- PeakFit counters invoke classic cases on advertisements as invitations to treat and non-binding marketing puff, including Partridge v Crittenden, Fisher v Bell, and Balfour v Balfour, pointing to practical and commercial policy reasons and their internal admission of promotional intent. — *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶3; *Balfour v Balfour* [1919] 2 KB 571, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6

## Issues

1. **I1.** Was the PeakFit advertisement a legally binding offer to Ms Hughes rather than mere invitation to treat?
2. **I2.** Is PeakFit's promise to pay £2,000 to Ms Hughes enforceable rather than merely marketing puff?
3. **I3.** Did PeakFit's term allowing withdrawal of promotions, not exercised before 15 April, affect the validity of Ms Hughes's contract?

## Key facts

- PeakFit published an advertisement on 5 January 2025 promising £2,000 to anyone completing 100 workouts and losing 5 kg by 15 April, backing it with £100,000 in Barclays. — Fact sheet ¶1
- The advertisement required participants to log all workouts and weigh in using the app's connected scales on 15 April. — Fact sheet ¶2
- Ms Hughes downloaded the app on 6 January, paid the subscription fee, completed all workouts, and weighed in on 15 April with a loss of 1.2 kg. — Fact sheet ¶3
- On 20 April, Ms Hughes claimed the £2,000 but PeakFit denied contractual obligation arguing the advertisement was only an invitation to treat and not an offer. — Fact sheet ¶4
- PeakFit's terms allowed withdrawal of promotions at any time, but no withdrawal occurred before 15 April. — Fact sheet ¶5
- More than 3,000 people participated, and PeakFit claims the advertisement was marketing puff, not a binding offer. — Fact sheet ¶6
- Ms Hughes aims to enforce the promise to pay £2,000 as a binding contract. — Fact sheet ¶7

## I1. Was the PeakFit advertisement a legally binding offer to Ms Hughes rather than mere invitation to treat?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256 | An advertisement can be a unilateral offer if it is clear, definite, and shows intention to be bound, demonstrated by a deposit to show sincerity; performance of conditions constitutes acceptance without notification. | *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204 | Advertisements are generally invitations to treat, not offers, due to impracticality and lack of intention to be bound to all respondants. | *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2 |
| *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394 | Displaying goods with prices is an invitation to treat, not an offer, which allows the seller to accept or reject offers from customers. | *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶3 |

**Argument for the client**

- PeakFit’s advertisement constituted a unilateral offer under Carlill, as it was clear, with defined conditions, and the £100,000 deposit indicated intention to be bound, so Ms Hughes' performance formed acceptance without need for notification. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3

**Argument for the other side**

- The advertisement was an invitation to treat rather than an offer, grounded in Partridge and Fisher which hold advertisements and displays are not offers due to their non-binding commercial nature, especially when thousands are expected to participate, reflecting PeakFit’s own expectation. — *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6

**Assessment**

- The Carlill precedent closely parallels the PeakFit scenario, particularly regarding the deposit demonstrating intention and acceptance by performance; while usual ad cases favour invitation to treat, the specific unilateral promise and deposited funds indicate an offer was made to Ms Hughes. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6

## I2. Is PeakFit's promise to pay £2,000 to Ms Hughes enforceable rather than merely marketing puff?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256 | A commercial promise supported by a deposit of money shows an intention to create legal relations and is enforceable when conditions are met, as in Carlill. | *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Balfour v Balfour* [1919] 2 KB 571 | Commercial or social promises without clear intention to create legal relations are unenforceable; there is a presumption against legal intention in domestic/social agreements like Balfour v Balfour. | *Balfour v Balfour* [1919] 2 KB 571, ¶2 |

**Argument for the client**

- The promise was enforceable given the commercial context, clear monetary deposit and defined conditions, demonstrating serious intention to create legal relations rather than marketing puff, consistent with Carlill’s binding unilateral contract. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3

**Argument for the other side**

- PeakFit’s promise was mere marketing puff lacking intention to create legal relations as indicated by PeakFit’s own denial of legal commitment and the high presumption against legal intention in informal promises as in Balfour v Balfour. — *Balfour v Balfour* [1919] 2 KB 571, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6

**Assessment**

- Though marketing puff is a common defense, the deposit and clear conditions strongly support intention to create legal relations, aligning with Carlill rather than Balfour, especially since this is a commercial promotion rather than a domestic/social agreement. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Balfour v Balfour* [1919] 2 KB 571, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6

## I3. Did PeakFit's term allowing withdrawal of promotions, not exercised before 15 April, affect the validity of Ms Hughes's contract?

**Supporting precedents**

- None retrieved.

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532 | Terms must be incorporated and known before or at contract conclusion to affect validity; notices appearing after contract formation are not part of the contract, per Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd. | *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3 |

**Argument for the client**

- The contract was concluded by Ms Hughes’ full performance on 15 April; PeakFit’s unilateral withdrawal right was not exercised and the term was not incorporated before formation, meaning the contract remains valid per Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

**Argument for the other side**

- PeakFit argues the withdrawal term negates contract formation since it was entitled to terminate the promotion before acceptance, and Ms Hughes did not notify her participation, aligning with the principle that late-noticed terms do not bind. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5

**Assessment**

- Given the unilateral nature of contract formation by full performance without notification, Ms Hughes accepted on 15 April, before which no withdrawal was made; thus, PeakFit’s withdrawal term was ineffective to prevent contract formation. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

## Conclusion

- The PeakFit advertisement operated as a unilateral offer supported by the deposited money showing intention to be bound, making Ms Hughes’ performance acceptance completing contract formation, following Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3
- PeakFit’s promise to pay £2,000 is enforceable and not mere marketing puff, considering the commercial scale, the clear offer conditions, and bona fide financial deposit, distinguishing it from non-binding social promises. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶1; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3
- The contractual term permitting withdrawal was not incorporated at or before contract formation and was not exercised prior to Ms Hughes’ completion of conditions, so it does not invalidate the contract or Ms Hughes' claim for payment. — *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶2; *Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd* [1949] 1 KB 532, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
