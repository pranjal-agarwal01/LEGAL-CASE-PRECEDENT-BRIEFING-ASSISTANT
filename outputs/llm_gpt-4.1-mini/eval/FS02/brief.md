# Brief: Okafor v St Aldhelm's Hospital NHS Trust

**Case** FS02 · **Acting for** Claimant · **Prepared** 15 November 2026 · **Mode** llm:foundry

## Summary

- Mr Okafor consulted Mr Lyle regarding a cervical decompression surgery recommended for his chronic neck pain, without being warned of a 2% risk of nerve damage or alternative treatments, despite his career depending on fine motor control of his left hand (F1-F3). — Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- Following the operation, Mr Okafor suffered permanent loss of fine motor control in two fingers, rendering him unable to work professionally (F5). — Fact sheet ¶5
- Mr Okafor claims damages for negligence due to failure to warn him of material risks and alternatives prior to surgery, asserting that this omission caused his loss (F8). — Fact sheet ¶8
- The opposing party relies on medical opinion evidence to argue non-negligence and lack of causation and remoteness of the claimed loss (F7). — Fact sheet ¶7

## Issues

1. **I1.** Did St Aldhelm's Hospital NHS Trust owe a duty of care to Mr Okafor regarding information about material risks and alternative treatments?
2. **I2.** Did Mr Lyle breach the standard of care by failing to warn Mr Okafor of the roughly 2% risk and alternative treatments before surgery?
3. **I3.** Was the risk of nerve damage a material risk requiring disclosure under informed consent principles?
4. **I4.** Did the failure to warn cause Mr Okafor's loss, considering he might have had the surgery eventually?
5. **I5.** Is the loss suffered by Mr Okafor recoverable as damage resulting from negligent omission to warn?

## Key facts

- On 2 February 2024, Mr Okafor consulted Mr Lyle about chronic neck pain radiating into his left arm. — Fact sheet ¶1
- Mr Lyle recommended cervical decompression surgery but did not mention a roughly 2% risk of nerve root damage or the alternative treatment of physiotherapy and steroid injections. — Fact sheet ¶2
- Mr Okafor informed Mr Lyle that his career depended on fine finger control of his left hand. — Fact sheet ¶3
- Mr Okafor signed a consent form and underwent surgery on 11 April 2024, which was performed with proper skill. — Fact sheet ¶4
- The risk of nerve damage materialized, causing permanent loss of fine motor control in two fingers of Mr Okafor's left hand, ending his professional career. — Fact sheet ¶5
- Mr Okafor says he would not have consented to surgery on that date if warned of the risk and would have tried physiotherapy and sought a second opinion, though he might have had surgery eventually. — Fact sheet ¶6
- The Trust relies on evidence that many responsible spinal surgeons in 2024 did not routinely mention the 2% risk and argues the surgery was not negligent, so failure to warn caused no loss. — Fact sheet ¶7
- Mr Okafor seeks damages for negligence based on failure to warn of material risk and alternative treatment before consenting to surgery. — Fact sheet ¶8

## I1. Did St Aldhelm's Hospital NHS Trust owe a duty of care to Mr Okafor regarding information about material risks and alternative treatments?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11 | A doctor owes a duty to take reasonable care to ensure the patient is aware of any material risks and reasonable alternatives before treatment. | *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶2 |
| *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562 | A duty of care arises where harm to a person is foreseeable and they are closely affected by the act or omission. | *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* [1957] 1 WLR 582 | The standard of care is judged by a responsible body of medical opinion, which might negate duty to warn if not customary practice. | *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* [1957] 1 WLR 582, ¶2 |

**Argument for the client**

- Montgomery establishes a clear duty on doctors to disclose material risks and reasonable alternatives to support patient autonomy (J06:p2). — *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶2
- The duty arises due to foreseeability of harm and proximity in the doctor-patient relationship, consistent with Lord Atkin’s neighbour principle in Donoghue (J01:p3). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3

**Argument for the other side**

- Bolam test supports that if a responsible body of surgeons does not customarily disclose a 2% risk, there may be no breach of duty in warning (J04:p2; F7). — *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* [1957] 1 WLR 582, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶7

**Assessment**

- Given the Supreme Court’s departure from Bolam for disclosure of risks in Montgomery, the hospital owed Mr Okafor a duty to inform him of the 2% risk and alternatives due to his specific concerns and the materiality of the risk (J06:p2, J06:p3, J01:p3). — *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶2; *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶3; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3

## I2. Did Mr Lyle breach the standard of care by failing to warn Mr Okafor of the roughly 2% risk and alternative treatments before surgery?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11 | Doctors must disclose material risks and alternatives relevant to the patient’s informed decision, regardless of accepted medical practice. | *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶2 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* [1957] 1 WLR 582 | Medical standard of care is based on a responsible body of professional opinion, which here supports not disclosing small risks routinely. | *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* [1957] 1 WLR 582, ¶2 |

**Argument for the client**

- Montgomery makes clear that failure to disclose a material risk and alternatives constitutes breach of the standard of care (J06:p2). — *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶2

**Argument for the other side**

- Since many spinal surgeons in 2024 do not routinely disclose this small risk, omission by Mr Lyle aligns with a responsible body of medical opinion and is not negligent under Bolam (J04:p2; F7). — *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* [1957] 1 WLR 582, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶7

**Assessment**

- Montgomery displaces Bolam’s deference in disclosure of risks; thus, failure to warn about a risk significant to Mr Okafor given his professional needs breaches the standard of care (J06:p2, J06:p3, F3). — *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶2; *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3

## I3. Was the risk of nerve damage a material risk requiring disclosure under informed consent principles?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11 | A risk is material if a reasonable person in the patient's position would likely attach significance to it, or if the doctor knows the patient would (J06:p3). | *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* [1957] 1 WLR 582 | Low probability risks accepted by a responsible medical body may not require disclosure (Bolam test). | *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* [1957] 1 WLR 582, ¶2 |
| *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850 | Very small probability of harm may not require precautions or warnings (J12:p2). | *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2 |

**Argument for the client**

- Given Mr Okafor’s career reliance on fine motor control, the 2% risk is material under Montgomery’s test (J06:p3; F3). — *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3

**Argument for the other side**

- The 2% risk is arguably too small to require disclosure, especially where it aligns with accepted professional practice (J12:p2; J04:p2; F7). — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* [1957] 1 WLR 582, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶7

**Assessment**

- Informed consent requires disclosure of risks material to the patient’s decision; Mr Okafor’s specific circumstances make this 2% risk material despite its low probability (J06:p3, F3). — *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3

## I4. Did the failure to warn cause Mr Okafor's loss, considering he might have had the surgery eventually?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41 | Liability arises for injury from a risk a doctor must warn about even if surgery would have occurred later, respecting patient autonomy (J07:p1, J07:p2). | *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶1; *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶2 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Barnett v Chelsea and Kensington Hospital Management Committee* [1969] 1 QB 428 | But-for causation requires proof that harm would not have occurred but for the breach, and injury would have occurred anyway (J08:p2). | *Barnett v Chelsea and Kensington Hospital Management Committee* [1969] 1 QB 428, ¶2 |

**Argument for the client**

- Chester v Afshar modifies but-for causation to hold doctor liable when undisclosed risks materialise, to vindicate patient autonomy (J07:p1, J07:p2). — *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶1; *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶2

**Argument for the other side**

- Mr Okafor’s admission that he might have had surgery later undermines causation, applying conventional but-for logic (J08:p2; F6). — *Barnett v Chelsea and Kensington Hospital Management Committee* [1969] 1 QB 428, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6

**Assessment**

- Following Chester, causation is established as the injury resulted from the very risk about which Mr Okafor was not warned, despite timing uncertainty (J07:p1, J07:p2, F6). — *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶1; *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6

## I5. Is the loss suffered by Mr Okafor recoverable as damage resulting from negligent omission to warn?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11 | Loss from undisclosed material risks is recoverable if reasonably foreseeable (J06:p2). | *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶2 |
| *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41 | Loss is not too remote where injury arises from material risk not disclosed, even if timing differs (J07:p3). | *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Overseas Tankship (UK) Ltd v Morts Dock and Engineering Co Ltd (The Wagon Mound No 1)* [1961] AC 388 | Liability is limited to reasonably foreseeable damage; loss from surgical risk without direct link to non-disclosure may be too remote (J09:p3). | *Overseas Tankship (UK) Ltd v Morts Dock and Engineering Co Ltd (The Wagon Mound No 1)* [1961] AC 388, ¶3 |

**Argument for the client**

- Montgomery and Chester affirm recovery for loss caused by failure to warn about material risks (J06:p2, J07:p3). — *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶2; *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶3

**Argument for the other side**

- The Wagon Mound principle limits liability to damage of a foreseeable kind; loss from injury due to accepted surgical risk may be too remote from omission (J09:p3; F5). — *Overseas Tankship (UK) Ltd v Morts Dock and Engineering Co Ltd (The Wagon Mound No 1)* [1961] AC 388, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

**Assessment**

- The loss of fine motor control is a foreseeable consequence of the undisclosed risk and thus recoverable as damages following Montgomery and Chester (J06:p2, J07:p3, F5). — *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶2; *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

## Conclusion

- Mr Okafor is owed a duty of care to be informed of material risks and reasonable alternatives before consenting to surgery, as required by Montgomery and supported by fundamental negligence principles (J06:p2; J01:p3). — *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶2; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3
- Mr Lyle breached the standard of care by failing to warn Mr Okafor of the specific 2% risk and alternative treatments, as such risks are material to a patient in Mr Okafor's position (J06:p2, J06:p3; F3). — *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶2; *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3
- The failure to warn caused Mr Okafor's loss, with causation established through the principle in Chester v Afshar protecting patient autonomy even if surgery might have occurred later (J07:p1, J07:p2; F6). — *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶1; *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6
- Therefore, the loss suffered by Mr Okafor is recoverable as foreseeable damage resulting from the negligent omission to warn, entitling him to damages in negligence (J06:p2, J07:p3; F5). — *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11, ¶2; *Chester v Afshar* [2004] UKHL 41, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
