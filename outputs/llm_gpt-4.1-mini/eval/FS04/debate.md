# Debate transcript: Rowe v Skyline Terraces Ltd

Mode: llm:foundry · LLM calls: 9

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I3] The cancellation of the fireworks is a frustrating event because it destroys the very foundation of the contract for both parties, akin to Krell v Henry where the cancelled processions frustrated the contract though the flat was still available. Here, both parties understood the terrace hire for the specific purpose of watching the fireworks, as evidenced by the advertising and correspondence, so the cancellation defeats the common purpose even if physical performance remained possible (F2, F3; J37:p2, J37:p3). — *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶2; *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A1.2** [I3] Unlike Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton, where frustration was denied because the naval review was only one of the purposes, in this case the fireworks were the sole basis for the contract recognized by both parties, not a separate commercial purpose of the claimant, making frustration applicable (F2, F5; J38:p2, J38:p3). — *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶2; *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A1.3** [I2] The absence of mention of the fireworks in the written contract does not negate their contractual significance, since the advertisement and email correspondence form part of the contract by showing mutual intention and clear promises, much like the unilateral offer and acceptance in Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co (F2, F3; J23:p2, J23:p3). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A1.4** [I4] Because the contract was frustrated by the cancellation of the fireworks, Mr Rowe is entitled to the return of his deposit and to be discharged from paying the balance, following Taylor v Caldwell where destruction of the essential subject discharged contractual obligations (F4, F7; J36:p2, J36:p3, J37:p2). — *Taylor v Caldwell* (1863) 3 B & S 826, ¶2; *Taylor v Caldwell* (1863) 3 B & S 826, ¶3; *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶7

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] Skyline had no duty of care to guarantee the fireworks display; the fireworks were an external event outside Skyline's control, so no proximity or reasonable foreseeability of harm to Mr Rowe arises. The police duty of care cases like Robinson v Chief Constable (J19) do not apply here, as Skyline's positive acts did not create a risk of physical injury or loss to Mr Rowe. — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶2; *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶3
- **C1.2** [I2] *(replies to A1.3)* The contract's written terms making no mention of the fireworks means no binding contractual obligation regarding the fireworks existed. The advertisement and emails are merely invitations to treat or sales puff, akin to Fisher v Bell (J50) and Partridge v Crittenden (J24), where advertisements were not offers. Without explicit incorporation, these do not form contract terms, undermining the claimant's reliance on them. — *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶2; *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶3; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C1.3** [I3] *(replies to A1.1)* The cancellation of the fireworks does not frustrate the contract because the contract grants exclusive use of the terrace with bar service, which was still available. Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton (J38) shows frustration does not apply where only one party's purpose is defeated, and performance, though less profitable, remains possible. Mr Rowe's personal commercial purpose cannot impose frustration on Skyline. — *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶2; *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C1.4** [I4] *(replies to A1.4)* Since no frustration has occurred, the deposit is not refundable upon cancellation by Mr Rowe, who chose to cancel despite performance being possible. The Taylor v Caldwell rule applies only when performance is impossible due to the subject-matter perishing. Here, the terrace and services existed and were provided. Mr Rowe's cancellation does not discharge payment obligations. — *Taylor v Caldwell* (1863) 3 B & S 826, ¶2; *Taylor v Caldwell* (1863) 3 B & S 826, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I3] *(replies to C1.3)* The cancellation of the fireworks is a frustrating event that discharges the contract because watching the fireworks was the foundation of the contract recognized by both parties, similar to Krell v Henry where cancellation of the coronation processions frustrated the underlying purpose despite the flat remaining available. Here, the mutual understanding of the parties, evidenced by emails and advertising, was that the terrace hire was for watching the fireworks, so frustration applies even though the terrace itself remained usable (F2, F3; J37:p2, J37:p3). — *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶2; *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A2.2** [I3] *(replies to C1.3)* Unlike Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton, where frustration was denied because only one party's purpose was defeated, here both parties had a common basis for contract founded on the fireworks event; it was not merely a commercial venture of one party, making frustration applicable (F2, F5; J38:p2, J38:p3). — *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶2; *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A2.3** [I2] *(replies to C1.2)* The absence of the fireworks mention in the written contract does not exclude their contractual significance, as the advertisement and Skyline's promise via email constitute a clear offer and mutual intention to contract on that basis, analogous to Carlill where an advertisement formed a binding unilateral offer (F2, F3; J23:p2, J23:p3). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A2.4** [I4] *(replies to C1.4)* Since the contract was frustrated by the cancellation of the fireworks, the deposit is refundable and the balance is not payable, consistent with Taylor v Caldwell where destruction of the essential subject discharged the contract and its obligations (F4, F7; J36:p2, J36:p3). — *Taylor v Caldwell* (1863) 3 B & S 826, ¶2; *Taylor v Caldwell* (1863) 3 B & S 826, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶7

## Round 2: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C2.1** [I1] Skyline owed no duty of care regarding the fireworks as they were an external event outside their control; unlike police duty cases such as Robinson, Skyline's acts did not create a foreseeable risk to Mr Rowe (J19:p2, J19:p3). — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶2; *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶3
- **C2.2** [I2] The contract's written terms omit any mention of fireworks, meaning the advertisement and emails are invitations to treat or sales puff, not binding offers as per Fisher v Bell and Partridge v Crittenden, so Mr Rowe cannot rely on them to add contractual obligations (J50:p2, J50:p3; J24:p2, J24:p3). — *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶2; *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶3; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶3
- **C2.3** [I3] Cancellation of the fireworks does not frustrate the contract since the terrace and bar remained available, making performance possible; Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton confirms frustration does not apply where only one party's purpose is defeated and performance can continue (J38:p2, J38:p3; F5). — *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶2; *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
- **C2.4** [I4] No frustration occurred, so Mr Rowe's cancellation does not entitle him to a refund; Taylor v Caldwell applies only where performance is impossible due to the subject perishing, but here the terrace and service existed and were provided (J36:p2, J36:p3; F5). — *Taylor v Caldwell* (1863) 3 B & S 826, ¶2; *Taylor v Caldwell* (1863) 3 B & S 826, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
- **C2.5** [I3] *(replies to A2.1)* Contrary to Krell v Henry, here the contract specifies only use of the terrace with bar service and the fireworks were not an express term, unlike the coronation procession which was the foundation of contract; thus cancellation of fireworks does not frustrate this contract (J37:p2, J37:p3; F3). — *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶2; *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C2.6** [I3] *(replies to A2.2)* Unlike the common basis in Krell, here the fireworks were only Mr Rowe's own commercial purpose, not a common foundation with Skyline, like in Herne Bay where frustration was denied as the contract's commercial venture was solely the claimant's purpose (J38:p2, J38:p3; F2). — *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶2; *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2
- **C2.7** [I2] *(replies to A2.3)* Carlill does not apply to this contract because advertisements can be invitations to treat, and here the crucial term about the fireworks was absent from the written contract, so the advertising promise was not incorporated into the binding agreement (J23:p2, J23:p3; J50:p2, J50:p3; F3). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶2; *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C2.8** [I4] *(replies to A2.4)* The refund of the deposit on frustration only arises if the contract is discharged by impossibility; but the terrace and services were provided and available so no frustration occurred and the deposit is rightly retained (J36:p2, J36:p3; F5). — *Taylor v Caldwell* (1863) 3 B & S 826, ¶2; *Taylor v Caldwell* (1863) 3 B & S 826, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
