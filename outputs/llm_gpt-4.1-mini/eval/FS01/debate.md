# Debate transcript: Nair v Brightwater Beverages Ltd

Mode: llm:foundry · LLM calls: 9

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] Brightwater owed Ms Nair a duty of care as a consumer under Donoghue v Stevenson because the drink was sold sealed and opaque, intended to reach the consumer without intermediate inspection, and Ms Nair was a foreseeable 'neighbour' under Lord Atkin’s principle, despite no contract with Brightwater. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶1; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A1.2** [I2] Brightwater breached the standard of care in manufacturing by allowing a sharp glass sliver and mould in a sealed bottle, which a reasonable manufacturer would have prevented by exercising care against such defects, judged objectively as in Nettleship v Weston and Paris v Stepney where vulnerability and potential severity raised required precautions. — *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶2; *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3; *Paris v Stepney Borough Council* [1951] AC 367, ¶2; *Paris v Stepney Borough Council* [1951] AC 367, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2
- **A1.3** [I3] The glass shard was the cause of Ms Nair's injury because, unlike Barnett, the injury was directly linked to the presence of glass, and under Smith v Leech Brain the defendant takes the claimant as found, including her pre-existing ulcer enhancing the harm, which does not negate causation. — *Barnett v Chelsea and Kensington Hospital Management Committee* [1969] 1 QB 428, ¶2; *Barnett v Chelsea and Kensington Hospital Management Committee* [1969] 1 QB 428, ¶3; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶2; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A1.4** [I4] Ms Nair's injuries are not too remote as the type of harm—a physical internal injury from glass—is foreseeable, and under Smith v Leech Brain and Donoghue v Stevenson the eggshell skull rule applies, meaning Brightwater is liable for the full extent of injury, including its increased severity due to her ulcer. — *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶2; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] *(replies to A1.1)* While Donoghue v Stevenson establishes a duty of care for manufacturers to ultimate consumers, the case does not rule out arguments about proximity and foreseeability in particular fact patterns, particularly where the claimant did not purchase the product themselves as in the present case where Ms Nair did not buy the kombucha, potentially limiting proximity under Caparo's incremental development principle (J01:p2; J02:p3; F1, F8). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶8
- **C1.2** [I2] *(replies to A1.2)* Brightwater's mere safety inspection on 1 March 2025 raises reasonable doubt about breach on an objective standard of care; the presence of the bottle's sealed cap prevents intermediate tampering, but the possibility of the cafe's intervention as Brightwater suggests creates uncertainty as to whether the defect was their breach (J12:p2; J14:p2; F2, F7). — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; *Latimer v AEC Ltd* [1953] AC 643, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶7
- **C1.3** [I3] *(replies to A1.3)* Causation is not established as the medical expert attributes most damage to the pre-existing ulcer, similar to Wilsher where multiple possible causes prevented proving causation on a balance of probabilities, and Ms Nair's worse injury could be due to her condition rather than the glass, thus breaking the causal chain (J22:p2; J08:p3; F5, F7). — *Wilsher v Essex Area Health Authority* [1988] AC 1074, ¶2; *Barnett v Chelsea and Kensington Hospital Management Committee* [1969] 1 QB 428, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7
- **C1.4** [I4] *(replies to A1.4)* Even if some injury occurred, the harm's remoteness should be recognized; The Wagon Mound principle limits liability to foreseeable kinds of damage and not all direct consequences, and the severity due to Ms Nair's ulcer is too remote and not reasonably foreseeable by Brightwater (J09:p3; J10:p3; F5). — *Overseas Tankship (UK) Ltd v Morts Dock and Engineering Co Ltd (The Wagon Mound No 1)* [1961] AC 388, ¶3; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* As held in Donoghue v Stevenson, a manufacturer owes a duty of care to the ultimate consumer when the product is sold sealed and opaque, intended to reach the consumer without reasonable possibility of intermediate examination; absence of contract does not bar this claim, directly applying here as Ms Nair, although not the purchaser, was the intended consumer of Brightwater's product (J01:p2, F2, F8, F1). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶8; Fact sheet ¶1
- **A2.2** [I2] *(replies to C1.2)* Brightwater breached the standard of care by allowing a sharp glass sliver and mould in a sealed bottle reaching Ms Nair, as a reasonable manufacturer would prevent such dangerous defects, judged objectively per Nettleship and heightened by known potential severity as in Paris v Stepney; Brightwater's passed inspection does not negate breach as the defect was present despite that, and the sealed cap excludes reasonable intermediate tampering reducing uncertainty (F2, F3, F7, J11:p3, J13:p3). — *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3; *Paris v Stepney Borough Council* [1951] AC 367, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7
- **A2.3** [I3] *(replies to C1.3)* Causation is established on the balance of probabilities because the injury directly followed ingestion of glass shard; under Smith v Leech Brain, Brightwater must take Ms Nair as found with her ulcer increasing harm extent, not breaking causation; the medical expert's suggestion does not suffice to prove the ulcer was the primary cause, contrasting Wilsher where multiple uncertain causes defeated causation (J10:p3, F3, F4, F5, F7). — *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7
- **A2.4** [I4] *(replies to C1.4)* Ms Nair’s injuries are not too remote because it is foreseeable that glass shards in a drink could cause internal injury; under The Wagon Mound principle, the type of harm was foreseeable and thus Brightwater is liable for the full extent of harm per the eggshell skull rule, including increased severity from her ulcer (J09:p3, J10:p3, F3, F5). — *Overseas Tankship (UK) Ltd v Morts Dock and Engineering Co Ltd (The Wagon Mound No 1)* [1961] AC 388, ¶3; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 2: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C2.1** [I1] *(replies to A2.1)* While Donoghue v Stevenson establishes a duty of care for manufacturers to ultimate consumers, this case does not automatically apply where the claimant did not purchase the product and lacks a direct contractual link; as noted in Caparo, imposition of a duty requires sufficient proximity and that it be fair, just and reasonable, which may not be satisfied here given Ms Nair did not buy the drink. Thus, the duty of care may be limited in this case (J02:p3, J01:p2, F1, F8). — *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶8
- **C2.2** [I2] *(replies to A2.2)* Brightwater's passing of a safety inspection days before the purchase raises a strong presumption of compliance with the standard of care. Moreover, the possibility of tampering by the cafe introduces significant uncertainty about whether the defect arose post-manufacture, distinguishing this case from cases where no intermediate interference is possible, thereby negating breach (J12:p2, J14:p2, F2, F7). — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; *Latimer v AEC Ltd* [1953] AC 643, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶7
- **C2.3** [I3] *(replies to A2.3)* Causation is not established because Ms Nair cannot prove on the balance of probabilities that the glass shard, rather than her pre-existing ulcer or other factors, caused the injuries, echoing Wilsher where multiple possible causes precluded causation. Hence, the causal chain is broken even if the glass shard was present (J22:p2, J08:p3, F5, F7). — *Wilsher v Essex Area Health Authority* [1988] AC 1074, ¶2; *Barnett v Chelsea and Kensington Hospital Management Committee* [1969] 1 QB 428, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7
- **C2.4** [I4] *(replies to A2.4)* Even if injury occurred, the harm is too remote as the law limits liability to foreseeable kinds of damage that are not disproportionate in extent; the extent of harm aggravated by Ms Nair’s ulcer is not a foreseeable or direct consequence of Brightwater’s actions, imposing a remoteness barrier to recovery (J09:p3, J10:p3, F5). — *Overseas Tankship (UK) Ltd v Morts Dock and Engineering Co Ltd (The Wagon Mound No 1)* [1961] AC 388, ¶3; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
