# Debate transcript: Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd v SwiftHaul Couriers Ltd

Mode: llm:foundry · LLM calls: 9

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] SwiftHaul owed a duty of care in the contractual performance to Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd, as the ordinary principles of negligence apply when positive acts create a foreseeable risk of harm (J19:p2). — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶2
- **A1.2** [I2] SwiftHaul breached the standard of care in delivery by sending the parcel to the wrong depot and delivering it eleven days late, failing to meet the guaranteed next-day delivery standard booked by the bakery (J11:p2,F4,F2). — *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶2
- **A1.3** [I3] The lost profits and lost wedding contract are recoverable since they arose naturally from the breach and the bakery's special circumstances were reasonably communicated by the parcel description and booking details (J35:p2,F2,F5). — *Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd* [1949] 2 KB 528, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A1.4** [I4] Clause 9 excluding liability for loss of profits is not enforceable as the clause was not sufficiently brought to the bakery's attention as an onerous or unusual term, given the online booking context and the bakery's failure to explicitly accept such broad exclusions (J45:p2,F3). — *Interfoto Picture Library Ltd v Stiletto Visual Programmes Ltd* [1989] QB 433, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A1.5** [I5] SwiftHaul could reasonably contemplate the loss of profits due to delay given the next-day guarantee and the electronic component nature of the parcel; their knowledge of the bakery's operational reliance and delivery urgency implies foreseeability of such losses (J35:p2,F2,F5). — *Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd* [1949] 2 KB 528, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] SwiftHaul did not owe a duty of care in negligence distinct from its contractual obligations, as there was no special relationship or proximity beyond contract (J02:p3). The delivery service was a commercial transaction unlikely to impose wider duties for purely economic loss. — *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3
- **C1.2** [I2] Although the parcel was delayed, the standard of care must be assessed by reasonableness and practicability of precautions; not every delay breaches the standard (J12:p3). Further, SwiftHaul's error may not be gross negligence but an isolated mistake not amounting to breach. — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶3
- **C1.3** [I3] Losses are recoverable only if arising naturally or within the parties' contemplation (J34:p2). Here, the bakery did not inform SwiftHaul of the special nature of the parcel; merely describing it as 'electronic component' is insufficient to communicate special circumstances for lost profits or the wedding contract (F2,F5). — *Hadley v Baxendale* (1854) 9 Exch 341, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
- **C1.4** [I4] Clause 9 was incorporated by the bakery's ticking the box on booking (F3), binding it to the terms (J40:p2). This clause clearly excludes lost profits, and in commercial contracts between parties of equal bargaining power such exclusions are generally upheld if clearly worded (J44:p3). — Fact sheet ¶3; *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶2; *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶3
- **C1.5** [I5] SwiftHaul could not reasonably contemplate the extraordinary lost wedding contract which was not communicated and exceptional (J34:p3,F5). The bakery’s failure to notify about its special reliance on the parcel means such loss was not within defendants' reasonable contemplation at contracting. — *Hadley v Baxendale* (1854) 9 Exch 341, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* SwiftHaul owed a duty of care in contractual performance as ordinary negligence principles apply where positive acts create reasonably foreseeable risk of harm, as held in Robinson where police owed duty in positive acts causing foreseeable injury (J19:p2). — *Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police* [2018] UKSC 4, ¶2
- **A2.2** [I2] *(replies to C1.2)* SwiftHaul breached the delivery standard by sending the parcel to the wrong depot and delivering eleven days late, failing to meet the guaranteed next-day service, which objectively was below the reasonable standard expected (J11:p2,F4,F2). — *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶2
- **A2.3** [I3] *(replies to C1.3)* The bakery can recover ordinary lost profits as these losses arose naturally from the breach given SwiftHaul knew the parcel was essential for immediate use, similar to Victoria Laundry where the seller knew the boiler was needed immediately for business (J35:p2,F2,F5). — *Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd* [1949] 2 KB 528, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A2.4** [I4] *(replies to C1.4)* Clause 9 excluding liability for loss of profits is unenforceable as it is onerous and unusual and was not fairly and reasonably brought to the bakery's attention in online booking, akin to Interfoto where a similar clause was not incorporated (J45:p2,F3). — *Interfoto Picture Library Ltd v Stiletto Visual Programmes Ltd* [1989] QB 433, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **A2.5** [I5] *(replies to C1.5)* SwiftHaul could reasonably contemplate the losses of ordinary profits given the guaranteed next-day delivery and the nature of the parcel, consistent with knowledge principle in Victoria Laundry that recovery depends on knowledge of circumstances at contracting (J35:p3,F2,F5). — *Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd* [1949] 2 KB 528, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 2: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C2.1** [I1] *(replies to A2.1)* SwiftHaul did not owe a duty of care separate from its contractual obligations since the relationship was purely commercial without proximity or a special relationship, as per Caparo's requirement of proximity and foreseeability which are absent here (J02:p3). — *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3
- **C2.2** [I2] *(replies to A2.2)* While there was a delay, the standard of care must consider likelihood and practicability; a single misrouting error may not amount to breach if reasonable steps were taken, referencing the low probability threshold in Bolton v Stone (J12:p3) distinguishing from learner driver negligence in Nettleship which is unrelated. — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶3; *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶2
- **C2.3** [I3] *(replies to A2.3)* Lost profits and the exceptional wedding contract are not recoverable since the bakery did not communicate the special circumstances, paralleling Hadley v Baxendale where the special reliance was unknown to the carrier (J34:p2), thus limiting damages to losses naturally arising in the ordinary course. — *Hadley v Baxendale* (1854) 9 Exch 341, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
- **C2.4** [I4] *(replies to A2.4)* Clause 9 excluding liability was incorporated by the bakery's ticking of the online acceptance box, binding them to its terms despite the lack of highlight, confirmed by L'Estrange where signature binds regardless of notice (J40:p2); thus, the exclusion applies to losses including lost profits (J44:p3). — *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶2; *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C2.5** [I5] *(replies to A2.5)* SwiftHaul could not reasonably foresee the extraordinary lost wedding contract as the bakery failed to notify any special circumstances, confining recoverability to losses arising in the ordinary course only (J34:p3); the contract breach was of a standard service with no communicated exceptional risk. — *Hadley v Baxendale* (1854) 9 Exch 341, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
