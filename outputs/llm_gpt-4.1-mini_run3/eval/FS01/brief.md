# Brief: Nair v Brightwater Beverages Ltd

**Case** FS01 · **Acting for** Claimant · **Prepared** 15 November 2026 · **Mode** llm:foundry

## Summary

- Ms Nair, who consumed a sealed kombucha bottle manufactured by Brightwater, suffered internal injuries from glass contamination, leading to hospitalization and loss of earnings (F1-F6). — Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6
- She has no direct contract with Brightwater, but claims negligence based on duty of care, breach of standard, causation including pre-existing vulnerability, and recoverability of consequential losses. — Fact sheet ¶8
- Authoritative precedents include Donoghue v Stevenson establishing manufacturer duty to ultimate consumers, Smith v Leech Brain endorsing 'take your victim as you find them' rule for causation, and Spartan Steel on consequential economic losses. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶2; *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶2

## Issues

1. **I1.** Does Brightwater owe a duty of care to Ms Nair despite her not purchasing the drink directly?
2. **I2.** Did Brightwater breach the standard of care in manufacturing and bottling the kombucha to prevent contamination?
3. **I3.** Is the pre-existing gastric ulcer a factor that affects causation of Ms Nair's injuries?
4. **I4.** Is Ms Nair's claim for losses and medical expenses recoverable as pure economic loss?

## Key facts

- Arjun Mehta bought two bottles of Fizzleaf kombucha and gave one to Ms Nair. — Fact sheet ¶1
- The bottle was sealed and made of dark brown glass, contents could not be inspected before opening. — Fact sheet ¶2
- Ms Nair drank half the bottle before noticing a sharp sliver of glass and mould at the bottom. — Fact sheet ¶3
- Ms Nair suffered severe abdominal pain and was admitted to hospital with internal lacerations. — Fact sheet ¶4
- Ms Nair has a pre-existing gastric ulcer condition that worsened the injury. — Fact sheet ¶5
- She was unable to work for several months and needed further medical procedures. — Fact sheet ¶6
- Brightwater claims the bottling line passed safety inspection and suggests possible tampering by the cafe; their medical expert attributes most damage to the ulcer. — Fact sheet ¶7
- Ms Nair did not purchase the drink herself and has no contractual relationship with Brightwater. — Fact sheet ¶8

## I1. Does Brightwater owe a duty of care to Ms Nair despite her not purchasing the drink directly?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562 | Manufacturer owes ultimate consumer a duty of care when products are sold sealed and intended to reach the consumer without intermediate examination. | *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605 | Duty requires proximity and it being fair, just and reasonable; challenged where product may have been tampered with or lack direct purchase contract. | *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶2; *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3 |

**Argument for the client**

- Under Donoghue v Stevenson, Brightwater owes a duty of care as the sealed bottle was intended to reach Ms Nair without intermediate examination, fulfilling the neighbour principle of foreseeability to ultimate consumers like Ms Nair (F1, F2, F3). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- The fact that Ms Nair did not buy the drink herself does not preclude duty, since it is well-settled that absence of contract is no barrier to a negligence claim against manufacturers (J01:p2). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2

**Argument for the other side**

- The possibility that the café tampered with the bottle breaks the chain of custody, undermining the directness required for duty under Donoghue; Caparo principles require fair, just and reasonable grounds for imposing duty, which is lacking here (F7). — *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶2; *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7

**Assessment**

- The facts strongly support the application of Donoghue's neighbour principle because the sealed bottle was unopened until consumption, making Brightwater liable despite no direct purchase. The alleged tampering is speculative and does not negate the duty owed by the manufacturer as the product left its control sealed. The Caparo criteria for novel duty impositions are met by this established scenario of manufacturer liability to consumers. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶7

## I2. Did Brightwater breach the standard of care in manufacturing and bottling the kombucha to prevent contamination?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562 | Manufacturers must take reasonable care to avoid contaminating products which could injure consumers. | *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2 |
| *Paris v Stepney Borough Council* [1951] AC 367 | Known vulnerabilities in consumers increase the required precaution under the standard of care. | *Paris v Stepney Borough Council* [1951] AC 367, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850 | A very low probability risk may justify no breach of duty; reasonable precautions may suffice even if harm eventually occurs. | *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2 |

**Argument for the client**

- Brightwater breached its duty in allowing glass and mould contamination in a sealed beverage; such hazards are wholly preventable and pose serious risks to consumers, mandating reasonable care (J01:p2; F3). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- Ms Nair's known gastric ulcer raises the standard of care as greater precautions are justified when harm consequences to certain consumers are more severe (J13:p3; F5). — *Paris v Stepney Borough Council* [1951] AC 367, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

**Argument for the other side**

- Brightwater's recent safety inspection confirms reasonable care; under Bolton v Stone, the low likelihood of contamination may absolve Brightwater of breach given proportionality of precautions (J12:p2; F7). — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶7

**Assessment**

- The contamination of glass and mould in a sealed bottle is a serious failure inconsistent with reasonable care expected from manufacturers. The known vulnerability of Ms Nair further heightens the duty. Brightwater's inspection record is insufficient to dispel liability given the actual presence of contamination and foreseeable harm. — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Paris v Stepney Borough Council* [1951] AC 367, ¶3; *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7

## I3. Is the pre-existing gastric ulcer a factor that affects causation of Ms Nair's injuries?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405 | The defendant takes the claimant as found and is liable for the full extent of harm once the type of harm is foreseeable, regardless of pre-existing conditions. | *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶2; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Wilsher v Essex Area Health Authority* [1988] AC 1074 | Where multiple causes exist and claimant cannot prove on the balance of probabilities that defendant's breach caused the harm, causation fails. | *Wilsher v Essex Area Health Authority* [1988] AC 1074, ¶2 |

**Argument for the client**

- Smith v Leech Brain applies the 'eggshell skull' rule; Brightwater must take Ms Nair as found, including her ulcer, and is liable for injury consequences once type of injury (lacerations from glass) was foreseeable (J10:p2, J10:p3; F3, F4, F5). — *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶2; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5

**Argument for the other side**

- The medical evidence suggests the ulcer caused most damage and multiple factors contributed to injury; under Wilsher, lack of proof that Brightwater's breach caused the damage on balance of probabilities negates liability (J22:p2; F5, F7). — *Wilsher v Essex Area Health Authority* [1988] AC 1074, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7

**Assessment**

- The defendant must take the claimant as they find them; Ms Nair's pre-existing ulcer does not break causation if the injury type was foreseeable. Though multiple factors exist, the initial injury by glass is attributable to Brightwater's breach and sufficiently causal to establish liability. — *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶2; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; *Wilsher v Essex Area Health Authority* [1988] AC 1074, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7

## I4. Is Ms Nair's claim for losses and medical expenses recoverable as pure economic loss?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27 | Economic loss consequential on physical damage is recoverable; losses flowing naturally from physical injury may be claimed. | *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶2 |

**Opposing precedents**

- None retrieved.

**Argument for the client**

- Ms Nair's lost earnings and medical expenses stem directly from physical injury caused by Brightwater's negligence, and thus qualify as consequential economic losses recoverable in negligence (J21:p2; F6). — *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6

**Argument for the other side**

- Brightwater argues the losses are akin to pure economic loss, which is generally unrecoverable unless directly consequent on physical property damage, and causation is disputed due to pre-existing conditions (J21:p2; F5, F6). — *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6

**Assessment**

- The losses claimed flow naturally from physical injuries and are not pure economic loss barred in negligence. Given causation is established, these consequential losses for medical expenses and lost earnings are recoverable against Brightwater. — *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6

## Conclusion

- Brightwater owed Ms Nair a duty of care as the sealed kombucha bottle was intended to reach the ultimate consumer without intermediate examination, consistent with Donoghue v Stevenson (I1). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2
- Brightwater breached the standard of care by failing to prevent glass and mould contamination, breaching the duty owed to consumers, particularly given Ms Nair's known vulnerability (I2). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Paris v Stepney Borough Council* [1951] AC 367, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
- Brightwater is liable for the full extent of Ms Nair's injuries under the 'eggshell skull' rule as the harm type was foreseeable, despite her pre-existing gastric ulcer (I3). — *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶2; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- Ms Nair's claims for medical expenses and lost earnings are recoverable consequential losses flowing from the physical injury Brightwater caused, not barred as pure economic loss (I4). — *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6
