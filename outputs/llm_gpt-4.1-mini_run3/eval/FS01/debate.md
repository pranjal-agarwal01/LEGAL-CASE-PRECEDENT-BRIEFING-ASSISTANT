# Debate transcript: Nair v Brightwater Beverages Ltd

Mode: llm:foundry · LLM calls: 9

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] Brightwater owes a duty of care to Ms Nair despite her not buying the drink herself because under Donoghue v Stevenson the manufacturer owes the ultimate consumer a duty to take reasonable care when products are sold in a sealed form intended to reach the consumer without intermediate examination, as here where the sealed bottle was opened only at the cafe (F1, F2). The neighbour principle demands foreseeability of harm to persons so closely affected by the product that they must be contemplated (J01:p2, J01:p3; F3, F4). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4
- **A1.2** [I2] Brightwater breached its standard of care by allowing kombucha bottles to contain glass and mould, hazards that a reasonable manufacturer must guard against especially as the product is sealed and intended for direct consumption. Donoghue confirms the manufacturer must take reasonable care to prevent harm to the consumer (J01:p2), and Paris v Stepney establishes that known vulnerabilities (Ms Nair's ulcer) increase required precautions (J13:p3; F3, F5). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Paris v Stepney Borough Council* [1951] AC 367, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A1.3** [I3] The pre-existing gastric ulcer does not absolve Brightwater of liability since under Smith v Leech Brain the defendant takes the claimant as found, liable for full extent of harm once the type of harm is foreseeable. Here, the internal lacerations and severe injury were foreseeable consequences of glass contamination (J10:p2, J10:p3; F3, F4, F5). — *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶2; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A1.4** [I4] Ms Nair's claims for lost earnings and medical expenses flow naturally from her physical injury caused by Brightwater's negligence, and are thus not pure economic loss barred by Caparo's foreseeability and proximity criteria. Losses consequential on physical injury are recoverable (J21:p2; F6). — *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] *(replies to A1.1)* Brightwater does not owe a duty of care to Ms Nair merely because she consumed the product, since Donoghue requires that the product be intended to reach the consumer without reasonable possibility of intermediate examination; here, the alleged tampering by the cafe undermines this crucial factor (F7). Moreover, Caparo imposes a requirement that it be fair, just and reasonable to impose a duty in novel situations, which is not demonstrated here by the facts (J02:p3). — *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶7
- **C1.2** [I2] *(replies to A1.2)* There is insufficient evidence that Brightwater breached its standard of care because its bottling line passed a safety inspection shortly before the alleged incident (F7). Moreover, Bolton v Stone teaches that a low probability of harm may justify no breach of standard, as precautions must be proportionate to risk; here, the risk of glass and mould contamination appears to have been minimal (J12:p2). — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶7
- **C1.3** [I3] *(replies to A1.3)* The pre-existing gastric ulcer impairs the causation claim, as Wilsher confirms that if the claimant cannot prove on the balance of probabilities that the defendant's breach caused the harm rather than other causes, liability fails; here, the medical expert's opinion that the ulcer caused most damage negates clear causation (J22:p2,F5,F7). — *Wilsher v Essex Area Health Authority* [1988] AC 1074, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7
- **C1.4** [I4] *(replies to A1.4)* Ms Nair's claim for lost earnings and medical expenses resembles pure economic loss, which is generally unrecoverable unless directly consequent on physical damage; Spartan Steel clarifies this limit, and here the link is attenuated because the injury might stem from pre-existing conditions and alternative causes (J21:p2,F6,F5). — *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* The sealed bottle of kombucha was intended to reach the ultimate consumer in the condition it left Brightwater's control, with no reasonable possibility of intermediate examination, fulfilling the conditions in Donoghue v Stevenson that establish a duty of care to Ms Nair despite her not purchasing directly (J01:p2, J01:p3; F1, F2). The allegation of tampering by the cafe does not negate Brightwater's duty as the manufacturer remains liable for safety in the product's original sealed state (J01:p2, F7). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶7
- **A2.2** [I2] *(replies to C1.2)* Brightwater breached the standard of care by allowing foreign glass and mould contamination in a sealed beverage, which a reasonable manufacturer must prevent given the foreseeable risk of serious injury to consumers; Donoghue imposes a duty to take reasonable care to avoid such contamination (J01:p2; F3). Paris v Stepney confirms that known vulnerabilities like Ms Nair's ulcer heighten the duty to take precautions, making the breach more significant here (J13:p3; F5). — *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562, ¶2; *Paris v Stepney Borough Council* [1951] AC 367, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A2.3** [I3] *(replies to C1.3)* Smith v Leech Brain confirms the defendant must take the claimant as found, and liability covers the full extent of harm caused by the injury once the type of harm is foreseeable, so Ms Nair's pre-existing ulcer does not break causation as the lacerations from glass contamination were foreseeable (J10:p2, J10:p3; F3, F4, F5). The medical expert's opinion does not negate causation where the initial injury is attributable to the contamination (F7). — *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶2; *Smith v Leech Brain & Co Ltd* [1962] 2 QB 405, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7
- **A2.4** [I4] *(replies to C1.4)* Ms Nair's losses for medical expenses and lost earnings flow naturally from the physical injury caused by the glass contamination, making them consequential losses recoverable under Spartan Steel; this is not pure economic loss barred by the Caparo test because the losses are directly tied to the physical damage suffered (J21:p2; F6). — *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6

## Round 2: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C2.1** [I1] *(replies to A2.1)* The presence of alleged tampering by the cafe means the bottle may not have reached Ms Nair in the sealed condition as from Brightwater, thus breaking the necessary chain for duty of care under Donoghue; the possibility of intermediate examination or interference removes the directness of the manufacturer's duty (F7). Further, Caparo requires proximity and that imposing a duty be fair, just and reasonable, which is lacking here due to lack of direct purchase and presence of an intermediary (J02:p2). — *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] 2 AC 605, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶7
- **C2.2** [I2] *(replies to A2.2)* Brightwater's passing of a safety inspection shortly before selling the product evidences reasonable precautions taken, and under Bolton v Stone, where the probability of harm is very low, the defendant is not negligent for failing to eliminate an unlikely risk of contamination (J12:p2; F7). The mere occurrence of contamination does not prove breach where procedures were reasonable and risks minor. — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶7
- **C2.3** [I3] *(replies to A2.3)* Ms Nair cannot establish causation on the balance of probabilities because the medical evidence indicates the pre-existing ulcer caused most of the damage, similar to Wilsher where multiple potential causes prevented liability; thus, the breach's causal contribution is insufficient (J22:p2; F5,F7). The claimant must prove the breach caused the injury, which here is unproven. — *Wilsher v Essex Area Health Authority* [1988] AC 1074, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7
- **C2.4** [I4] *(replies to A2.4)* Ms Nair's lost earnings and medical expenses resemble pure economic loss not recoverable beyond physical damage itself, as Spartan Steel holds loss must be consequential on physical damage, and here the causal chain is weakened by disputed causation and pre-existing conditions (J21:p2; F6,F5). The claim for earnings lost from prolonged incapacity is too remote or indirect for recovery. — *Spartan Steel & Alloys Ltd v Martin & Co (Contractors) Ltd* [1973] QB 27, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6; Fact sheet ¶5
