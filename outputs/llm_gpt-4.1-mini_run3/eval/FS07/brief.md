# Brief: Brennan Joinery Ltd v Castlegate Developments Ltd

**Case** FS07 · **Acting for** Claimant · **Prepared** 15 November 2026 · **Mode** llm:foundry

## Summary

- The case revolves around whether Brennan Joinery Ltd can claim an additional £18,000 promised by Castlegate Developments Ltd for completing kitchen and staircase fitments on time under an alleged contract variation. — Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6
- Brennan argues that the promise of extra payment is supported by consideration because Castlegate obtained a practical benefit by avoiding costly penalties and the trouble of engaging another contractor, per Williams v Roffey Bros (J32:p3). — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6
- Castlegate counters that Brennan was already contractually obliged to complete the work on time, so no fresh consideration supports the promise, invoking Stilk v Myrick (J31:p3) and asserting the promise is gratuitous absent economic duress. — *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶6
- On offer and acceptance, Brennan likens Castlegate's promise to a unilateral offer accepted by performance under Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co (J23:p2), whereas Castlegate argues the promise was a mere negotiation lacking clear acceptance. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5

## Issues

1. **I1.** Is there a valid variation to the contract allowing Brennan Joinery Ltd to claim the extra £18,000 from Castlegate Developments Ltd?
2. **I2.** Does Castlegate Developments Ltd's offer of additional payment to Brennan Joinery Ltd amount to a valid contract modification under the principles of offer and acceptance?
3. **I3.** Is Castlegate Developments Ltd's refusal to pay the extra £18,000 a breach of contract?

## Key facts

- Brennan was engaged by Castlegate in January 2025 to complete work on 30 townhouses for a fixed price of £150,000 by 30 June 2025. — Fact sheet ¶1
- Castlegate's contract with the developer includes a liquidated damages clause of £5,000 per day for late completion. — Fact sheet ¶2
- By April 2025, Brennan had completed 12 houses, but stated financial difficulties and possible inability to finish the project. — Fact sheet ¶3
- On 14 April 2025, Castlegate promised in writing to pay Brennan an additional £1,000 per remaining house completed on time to avoid penalties or hiring another joiner. — Fact sheet ¶4
- Brennan completed the remaining 18 houses by the deadline of 30 June 2025. — Fact sheet ¶5
- Castlegate paid only the original contract price but refused to pay the extra £18,000, claiming Brennan was already obligated to complete the work under the original contract. — Fact sheet ¶5
- Brennan did not threaten to abandon the contract to obtain the extra payment; the extra payment offer originated from Castlegate. — Fact sheet ¶6
- Brennan seeks to recover the promised £18,000 in additional payment. — Fact sheet ¶7

## I1. Is there a valid variation to the contract allowing Brennan Joinery Ltd to claim the extra £18,000 from Castlegate Developments Ltd?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1 | A promise of extra payment for performing an existing contractual duty is enforceable if the promisor obtains a practical benefit and there is no economic duress. | *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3 |
| *Hyde v Wrench* (1840) 3 Beav 334 | A valid acceptance must be clear; offers and counter-offers must be correctly understood to form valid contracts. | *Hyde v Wrench* (1840) 3 Beav 334, ¶1; *Hyde v Wrench* (1840) 3 Beav 334, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317 | Performing an existing contractual duty owed to the promisor is not good consideration for a promise of extra payment, making such promises unenforceable. | *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3 |

**Argument for the client**

- Under Williams v Roffey Bros, Castlegate obtained a practical benefit—avoiding a £5,000 daily penalty and disruption—which suffices as consideration for the promise of extra payment, with no signs of economic duress present. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6
- The Stilk v Myrick rule is refined rather than overruled; where a practical benefit beyond the original contract exists, consideration supports the variation, as is the case here. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4

**Argument for the other side**

- Stilk v Myrick strictly bars enforcement of extra payment promises based solely on performing pre-existing contractual duties, and Brennan was already contractually bound to complete the work on time. — *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶5
- Williams v Roffey Bros does not extend to promises that merely avoid a penalty the promisor is already contractually liable for, rendering the promise gratuitous absent economic duress or fraud. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6

**Assessment**

- The practical benefit doctrine in Williams v Roffey offers strong support for recognizing the variation as valid since Castlegate avoided substantial penalties and disruption, and no duress was involved. While Stilk v Myrick provides a strict rule against enforcement without fresh consideration, the practical benefit exception is well established and applicable here given the facts. Therefore, Brennan likely has valid consideration supporting the contract variation. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6

## I2. Does Castlegate Developments Ltd's offer of additional payment to Brennan Joinery Ltd amount to a valid contract modification under the principles of offer and acceptance?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256 | Clear unilateral offers can be accepted by performance, forming binding contract modifications without need for notification of acceptance. | *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Hyde v Wrench* (1840) 3 Beav 334 | A counter-offer or lack of unequivocal acceptance can prevent contract modification; mere performance of existing duties without clear acceptance does not bind the promisor. | *Hyde v Wrench* (1840) 3 Beav 334, ¶3 |
| *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317 | Performance of an existing contractual duty does not amount to acceptance of a new contractual modification without fresh consideration or clear mutual assent. | *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3 |

**Argument for the client**

- Castlegate's email promising £1,000 extra per house is a clear unilateral offer intended to be binding upon performance, accepted when Brennan completed the houses on schedule, analogous to Carlill where performance suffices for acceptance. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- The promise was definite and binding, thus amounting to contract modification by offer and acceptance, with no rejection or counter-offer that would negate the original offer. — *Hyde v Wrench* (1840) 3 Beav 334, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4

**Argument for the other side**

- Castlegate's promise is better characterized as a mere negotiation or unenforceable promise lacking fresh consideration, not a binding unilateral offer, especially as Brennan was performing pre-existing obligations. — *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; *Hyde v Wrench* (1840) 3 Beav 334, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶4
- Unlike Carlill, which was a public offer made to anyone who performed, here the promise was not communicated as a binding offer and lacked the certainty and communication required for contract modification. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5

**Assessment**

- The analogy to Carlill supports treating Castlegate's email as a unilateral offer accepted by performance, establishing a contract modification. Opposing arguments hinge on strict offer and acceptance principles limiting modification absent fresh consideration, yet the facts here show clear intent and acceptance. Therefore, the evidence favors a valid contract modification under offer and acceptance doctrines. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Hyde v Wrench* (1840) 3 Beav 334, ¶3; *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5

## I3. Is Castlegate Developments Ltd's refusal to pay the extra £18,000 a breach of contract?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1 | Once a valid contract variation is formed by promise of extra payment and performance, refusal to pay the promised amount is a breach of the modified contract. | *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶1; *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317 | If no valid contract variation exists, refusal to pay extra for performance of existing obligations is not a breach; original contract terms prevail. | *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶2 |

**Argument for the client**

- Assuming the variation is valid under Williams v Roffey, Brennan performed the additional obligations as promised, so Castlegate's refusal to pay constitutes breach of the contract variation. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶1; *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

**Argument for the other side**

- Absent a valid modification, refusal to pay extra cannot be breach, as Brennan only performed work required under the original contract and Castlegate's refusal is consistent with contract terms. — *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5

**Assessment**

- If the contract variation is upheld, refusal to pay Brennan the extra £18,000 is a clear breach of contract. Since the evidence and relevant precedent strongly support validity of the variation, the breach claim is well founded. Conversely, without valid variation, no breach arises. Overall, the breach claim is compelling if the first two issues are decided for Brennan. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶1; *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5

## Conclusion

- Brennan Joinery Ltd is likely entitled to enforce the promise of additional payment based on the practical benefit doctrine in Williams v Roffey, establishing valid consideration for contract variation despite existing obligations. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6
- Castlegate's promise constitutes a valid unilateral offer accepted by performance, amounting to contract modification under offer and acceptance principles analogous to Carlill. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- Accordingly, Castlegate's refusal to pay the extra £18,000 constitutes a breach of the modified contract, entitling Brennan to recover the promised amount. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶1; *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
