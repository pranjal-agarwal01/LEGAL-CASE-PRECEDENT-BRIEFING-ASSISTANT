# Debate transcript: Brennan Joinery Ltd v Castlegate Developments Ltd

Mode: llm:foundry · LLM calls: 9

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] Under Williams v Roffey Bros (J32:p3), a promise of extra payment for performing an existing contractual duties can be supported by consideration if the promisor obtains a practical benefit, such as Castlegate avoiding the developer's penalties and trouble of finding another joiner, fitting closely with these facts (F2,F4,F5). There was no economic duress or fraud (F6), so the variation promise is enforceable. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6
- **A1.2** [I2] The promise by Castlegate on 14 April 2025 to pay extra per house was a clear offer intended to be legally binding, accepted by Brennan's completion of the remaining houses on schedule, analogous to a unilateral offer accepted by performance as in Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co (J23:p2). This establishes a valid contract modification by offer and acceptance. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A1.3** [I3] Castlegate's refusal to pay the £18,000 extra amounts to breach of the contract variation, since under Williams v Roffey (J32:p1,p3) Brennan performed the additional obligations and obtained the promised payment, making the original contract terms varied and enforceable (F5). — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶1; *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] *(replies to A1.1)* The promise of extra payment to Brennan is not enforceable as good consideration is lacking because Brennan was already contractually obliged to complete the works on time; this follows Stilk v Myrick (J31:p3), which strictly holds that performing an existing contractual duty is not good consideration. — *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶5
- **C1.2** [I1] *(replies to A1.1)* Williams v Roffey Bros (J32:p3) merely refines Stilk v Myrick and cannot be applied to extend consideration where the promisor's practical benefit is merely avoiding a penalty already contractually due, which is not a new benefit, especially given Brennan did not threaten to desist (F6), so the promise remains gratuitous. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C1.3** [I2] *(replies to A1.2)* Castlegate's email promise of a bonus cannot be a valid unilateral offer binding on them as it is more properly seen as a negotiation or an unenforceable promise unsupported by fresh consideration, since the original contract already mandated timely completion (F1,F4); Carlill (J23:p2) is readily distinguishable as it involved an offer to the world not a bilateral contract variation. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶4
- **C1.4** [I2] *(replies to A1.2)* Unlike Carlill, here Brennan did not accept a clear unilateral offer but merely proceeded as contractually obliged; hence no valid contract modification occurred under offer and acceptance principles (J26:p3), as Castlegate's promise was a mere attempt to modify an existing contract without new consideration. — *Hyde v Wrench* (1840) 3 Beav 334, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶4
- **C1.5** [I3] *(replies to A1.3)* Castlegate's refusal to pay cannot amount to breach of a contract variation because no valid modification existed; Brennan's claim to the £18,000 is unsupported by enforceable variation due to absence of consideration (J31:p2) and the original obligations remain intact (F5). — *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* Under Williams v Roffey Bros (J32:p3), the promise of extra payment for performing existing contractual duties is enforceable if the promisor obtains a practical benefit, which here includes Castlegate avoiding the developer's penalty and the trouble of finding another joiner (F2, F4). There was no economic duress or fraud (F6), satisfying the conditions for consideration and making the variation valid. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶6
- **A2.2** [I1] *(replies to C1.2)* The Stilk v Myrick rule (J31:p3) is refined, not overruled by Williams; where practical benefits flow to the promisor beyond the original contract terms, consideration exists. Here, the practical benefit is avoiding a large penalty and disruption, which is a genuine new benefit, unlike the opponent's claim (F2, F4). — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4
- **A2.3** [I2] *(replies to C1.3)* Castlegate's email promising extra payment on 14 April 2025 was a clear unilateral offer intended to induce performance, accepted by Brennan completing the additional houses on time, analogous to Carlill (J23:p2) where performance constituted acceptance without need for notification (F4, F5). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- **A2.4** [I2] *(replies to C1.4)* The opponent wrongly characterises Castlegate's promise as mere negotiation; the promise was definite and binding upon performance, amounting to contract modification by offer and acceptance, unlike Hyde where a counter-offer ended negotiations (J26:p3) and here there was no rejection or counter-offer (F4). — *Hyde v Wrench* (1840) 3 Beav 334, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4
- **A2.5** [I3] *(replies to C1.5)* As the variation promise was valid and relied upon by Brennan (J32:p1, p3), Castlegate's refusal to pay the extra £18,000 is a breach of the modified contract (F5), since the original terms were varied and performance beyond original obligations was rendered enforceable. — *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶1; *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

## Round 2: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C2.1** [I1] *(replies to A2.1)* The strict rule in Stilk v Myrick bars the promise of additional payment for duties the promisor is already contractually bound to perform, as Brennan was already obliged to complete the houses on time (F1), so no fresh consideration exists. — *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶1
- **C2.2** [I1] *(replies to A2.2)* Williams v Roffey does not override Stilk but only refines it; here the 'practical benefit' of avoiding a penalty is not a new benefit but merely the promisor avoiding a cost it is already contractually liable for, rendering the promise gratuitous especially absent economic duress (F2, F6). — *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶3; *Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd* [1991] 1 QB 1, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C2.3** [I2] *(replies to A2.3)* Castlegate's email is better characterised as a mere promise or negotiation, lacking the certainty and communication of acceptance required for a unilateral offer acceptance contract modification, unlike the clear offer in Carlill which was made to the world and unequivocally accepted by performance (F4, F5). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- **C2.4** [I2] *(replies to A2.4)* In contrast to Hyde, where a counter-offer extinguished the original offer, here Brennan did not unequivocally accept a new offer; simply performing pre-existing duties does not amount to acceptance of a contract variation under strict offer and acceptance rules (J26:p3, F4). — *Hyde v Wrench* (1840) 3 Beav 334, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4
- **C2.5** [I3] *(replies to A2.5)* Castlegate's refusal to pay extra cannot be breach of a valid variation since no binding variation existed; Brennan's performance was only of original contractual duties without fresh consideration, so the original contract terms still control (J31:p2, F5). — *Stilk v Myrick* (1809) 2 Camp 317, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5
