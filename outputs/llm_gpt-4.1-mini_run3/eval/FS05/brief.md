# Brief: Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd v SwiftHaul Couriers Ltd

**Case** FS05 · **Acting for** Claimant · **Prepared** 15 November 2026 · **Mode** llm:foundry

## Summary

- The case concerns breach of contract by SwiftHaul for delayed delivery of a critical electronic component to Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd, causing loss of ordinary and special profits. — Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5
- The objective standard of care requires SwiftHaul to deliver timely as a reasonably competent carrier, which it failed by sending the parcel 11 days late. — *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4
- Lost ordinary profits are recoverable where the defendant knew the goods were for immediate use in business, but exceptional losses unknown to the defendant are not recoverable. — *Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd* [1949] 2 KB 528, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6
- Clause 9 excluding liability for loss of profits was accepted by the bakery but may be subject to strict construction and fairness considerations in light of the particular facts and undisclosed impact. — *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶2; *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶2; *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

## Issues

1. **I1.** Is SwiftHaul liable for breach of contract for the delayed delivery of the electronic component to Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd?
2. **I2.** Are Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd's lost profits recoverable as damages for SwiftHaul's breach of contract?
3. **I3.** Does Clause 9 of SwiftHaul's terms and conditions validly exclude liability for loss of profits and the one-off contract claimed by Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd?

## Key facts

- The bakery's only industrial oven controller board failed on 3 September 2025, requiring repair at a workshop in Birmingham. — Fact sheet ¶1
- On 4 September 2025, the bakery booked SwiftHaul's next-day delivery service online, describing the parcel only as 'electronic component' and not informing SwiftHaul it was essential for trading. — Fact sheet ¶2
- The bakery's manager accepted SwiftHaul's terms and conditions, including an exclusion clause (Clause 9) excluding liability for loss of profits arising from delay. — Fact sheet ¶3
- SwiftHaul erroneously sent the parcel to the wrong depot, resulting in delivery eleven days late on 15 September 2025. — Fact sheet ¶4
- The bakery lost twelve days of baking activity, causing about £18,000 in lost ordinary trade profits and a £40,000 one-off contract to supply cakes, unknown to SwiftHaul. — Fact sheet ¶5
- SwiftHaul argues it could not have contemplated the bakery's inability to trade, the exceptional nature of the wedding contract, and relies on Clause 9 to exclude liability for these losses. — Fact sheet ¶6
- The bakery claims damages for breach of contract including lost profits and the lost wedding contract. — Fact sheet ¶7

## I1. Is SwiftHaul liable for breach of contract for the delayed delivery of the electronic component to Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691 | The standard of care in negligence is objective and does not vary with the defendant's own limitations; a defendant is held to the standard of a reasonably competent person doing it. | *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850 | Foreseeability alone does not establish breach; the reasonable person may disregard a very small risk, weighing probability and practicability of precautions. | *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2 |

**Argument for the client**

- SwiftHaul breached its contractual duty by delivering the parcel 11 days late due to a dispatch error, falling below the objective standard of a reasonably competent carrier. — *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4

**Argument for the other side**

- The probability of loss due to delay is low and reasonable precautions were taken; foreseeability alone is insufficient for breach under Bolton v Stone. — *Bolton v Stone* [1951] AC 850, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4

**Assessment**

- Given the clear error in delivery timing and the objective standard requiring a reasonably competent carrier, SwiftHaul is liable for breach. The low probability reasoning is less persuasive here as the breach was a fundamental failure to timely deliver. — *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4

## I2. Are Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd's lost profits recoverable as damages for SwiftHaul's breach of contract?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd* [1949] 2 KB 528 | Ordinary lost profits are recoverable if the defendant knew the goods were for immediate business use; special profits unknown to the defendant are not recoverable. | *Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd* [1949] 2 KB 528, ¶2 |

**Opposing precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *Hadley v Baxendale* (1854) 9 Exch 341 | Losses from special circumstances not communicated to the defendant are not recoverable; damages cover loss naturally arising or within the contemplation of both parties at contracting. | *Hadley v Baxendale* (1854) 9 Exch 341, ¶2 |

**Argument for the client**

- The bakery's ordinary lost profits are recoverable since SwiftHaul knew the component was for immediate business use, paralleling Victoria Laundry; only the exceptional wedding contract profits are unrecoverable due to lack of notice. — *Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd* [1949] 2 KB 528, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6

**Argument for the other side**

- Lost profits are not recoverable because the bakery did not communicate the special circumstance that it could not trade without the component; Hadley v Baxendale bars such unnotified special losses. — *Hadley v Baxendale* (1854) 9 Exch 341, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2

**Assessment**

- The bakery can recover ordinary lost profits as SwiftHaul knew the component was critical for immediate use, but special contract losses are likely unrecoverable due to lack of notice of those special circumstances. — *Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd* [1949] 2 KB 528, ¶2; *Hadley v Baxendale* (1854) 9 Exch 341, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5

## I3. Does Clause 9 of SwiftHaul's terms and conditions validly exclude liability for loss of profits and the one-off contract claimed by Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd?

**Supporting precedents**

| Case | Proposition | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394 | A party who signs a contract is bound by its terms absent fraud or misrepresentation regardless of reading. | *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶2 |
| *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416 | Unsigned terms on a ticket or notice are incorporated if reasonable steps were taken to bring them to attention before or at contract formation. | *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶2 |
| *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827 | In commercial contracts between parties of equal bargaining power who allocate risks, clearly worded exclusion clauses will be enforced according to their true construction. | *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶3 |

**Opposing precedents**

- None retrieved.

**Argument for the client**

- The bakery accepted clause 9 by ticking the online acceptance box, binding them to it; reasonable steps brought the clause to their attention, analogous to signing and notice cases. — *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶2; *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- As a commercial contract between parties of equal bargaining power, the exclusion clause should be enforced per its clear wording, excluding liability for loss of profit despite undisclosed loss scale. — *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

**Argument for the other side**

- Despite acceptance, the clause should be strictly construed and penalized if unfair due to the bakery's lack of knowledge of the clause's wide impact; fairness considerations may limit its scope. — *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

**Assessment**

- Clause 9 is validly incorporated and binding. Although SwiftHaul may argue strict enforcement, fairness and undisclosed circumstances warrant strict construction, potentially limiting exclusion of liability for losses claimed by the bakery. — *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶2; *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶2; *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5

## Conclusion

- SwiftHaul is liable for breach of contract for delaying the delivery eleven days beyond the guaranteed time, failing to meet the objective standard of a reasonably competent carrier. — *Nettleship v Weston* [1971] 2 QB 691, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶4
- Kaur Artisan Bakery Ltd can recover ordinary lost profits because SwiftHaul knew the component's purpose for immediate use; however, special contract losses unknown to SwiftHaul are not recoverable. — *Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd* [1949] 2 KB 528, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶1; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6
- Clause 9 excluding liability is incorporated by acceptance but should be strictly construed under fairness principles, potentially limiting SwiftHaul's exclusion of liability for the claimed losses. — *L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd* [1934] 2 KB 394, ¶2; *Parker v South Eastern Railway Co* (1877) 2 CPD 416, ¶2; *Photo Production Ltd v Securicor Transport Ltd* [1980] AC 827, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
