# Debate transcript: Rowe v Skyline Terraces Ltd

Mode: llm:foundry · LLM calls: 9

## Round 1: Argument Agent (client)

- **A1.1** [I1] The contract for hiring the terrace was frustrated by the cancellation of the fireworks, as the advertised key purpose and common foundation of the contract was to view the fireworks, which both parties recognised (F2). In Krell v Henry, the cancellation of the King's coronation processions, which was the foundation of the contract though not in the written terms, frustrated the contract and released the hirer from payment obligation (J37:p1-J37:p3). Here, although the terrace remained available, the supervening event destroyed the fundamental basis of the contract, making this a classic frustration case. — *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶1; *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶2; *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2
- **A1.2** [I2] The promise of 'front-row seats for the display' in Skyline's reply emails constituted a binding term or collateral contract about the fireworks view, as it was a definitive assurance that was essential to Mr Rowe's purpose (F2). This aligns with the principles in Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co, where a clear offer with intent to be bound is established by unequivocal terms and accompanying communication, even if not in the main written agreement (J23:p2-J23:p3). This term affects the parties' obligations as Mr Rowe's performance was contingent on this feature. — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2
- **A1.3** [I3] Because the contract was frustrated, Mr Rowe is entitled to recover his deposit and avoid payment of the balance, as the supervening event excused performance and terminated the contract obligations. Following Krell v Henry, the hirer was released from payment where the event foundational to the contract was cancelled (J37:p2). Since the contract cannot be performed as originally intended, retention of the deposit by Skyline would be unjustified. — *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶7

## Round 1: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C1.1** [I1] *(replies to A1.1)* The cancellation of the fireworks does not frustrate the contract as performance of the contract remains possible with the terrace, bar, and staff available, so the purpose of the contract is not destroyed but merely less attractive, as in Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton where the contract was not frustrated despite cancellation of the naval review (J38:p1, J38:p2, J38:p3, F5). — *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶1; *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶2; *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5
- **C1.2** [I2] *(replies to A1.2)* Skyline's online advertisement and the email promise of 'front-row seats' are merely invitations to treat or statements of opinion rather than binding contractual terms, akin to the holdings in Fisher v Bell and Partridge v Crittenden, which state that advertisements and invitations to treat are not offers, preventing the formation of a collateral contract (J50:p2, J50:p3, J24:p2, J24:p3, F2). Moreover, the written hire agreement does not include any term about the fireworks, which further weakens any claim of a collateral contract (F3). — *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶2; *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶3; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C1.3** [I3] *(replies to A1.3)* Since the contract was not frustrated and remains capable of performance, Skyline is entitled to retain the deposit and claim the balance, as the risk that the event became less attractive lies with Mr Rowe, similar to the principle in Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton and Davis Contractors Ltd where hardship or loss alone does not justify contract termination or recovery of deposits (J38:p3, J39:p2, J39:p3, F6). — *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶3; *Davis Contractors Ltd v Fareham Urban District Council* [1956] AC 696, ¶2; *Davis Contractors Ltd v Fareham Urban District Council* [1956] AC 696, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6

## Round 2: Argument Agent (client)

- **A2.1** [I1] *(replies to C1.1)* The cancellation of the fireworks fundamentally frustrates the contract because the common foundation, recognised by both parties, was the fireworks viewing; this is analogous to Krell v Henry where the cancelled coronation processions frustrated the contract even though the physical venue remained available for use (J37:p1, J37:p2, J37:p3, F2, F4). Unlike Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton, where an alternative purpose remained, here the contract was based solely on the fireworks display, making continued performance radically different from what was agreed (F2, F4). — *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶1; *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶2; *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4
- **A2.2** [I2] *(replies to C1.2)* Skyline's specific promise of 'front-row seats for the display' in emails constituted a binding collateral contract about the fireworks, meeting the criteria in Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co for a clear offer with an intention to be bound, especially as Mr Rowe relied on this assurance and it was central to the contract's purpose (J23:p2, J23:p3, F2). This contrasts with mere advertisements or invitations to treat as in Fisher v Bell and Partridge v Crittenden, since here the promise was made directly in response to Mr Rowe's inquiry and formed part of the parties' agreement (F2). — *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶2; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* [1893] 1 QB 256, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2
- **A2.3** [I3] *(replies to C1.3)* Due to frustration, Mr Rowe is entitled to recover his deposit and avoid paying the balance, as performance became impossible in the contractual sense and the risk of cancellation falls on Skyline; per Krell v Henry, the hirer is released from payment when the foundational event is cancelled, precluding retention of the deposit (J37:p2, F4, F7). This contrasts with cases like Herne Bay and Davis Contractors where frustration was not found as performance was still possible or only made less profitable (J38:p3, J39:p2, J39:p3, F5, F6). — *Krell v Henry* [1903] 2 KB 740, ¶2; Fact sheet ¶4; Fact sheet ¶7

## Round 2: Counter-Argument Agent (other side)

- **C2.1** [I1] *(replies to A2.1)* The contract was not frustrated as the terrace, bar, and staff remained available, so performance was possible and the contract was not radically different, akin to Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton, where cancellation of the naval review did not frustrate the contract because a cruise round the fleet was still possible, and the cancelled event was merely a commercial purpose of the charterer (J38:p2, J38:p3, F5, F6). — *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶2; *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶5; Fact sheet ¶6
- **C2.2** [I2] *(replies to A2.2)* The online listing and email promise of 'front-row seats' constitute invitations to treat or mere representations, not a binding collateral contract, paralleling Fisher v Bell and Partridge v Crittenden, where advertisements were not offers; the written agreement which is the main contract omits any fireworks term, further negating a collateral contract (J50:p2, J50:p3, J24:p2, J24:p3, F2, F3). — *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶2; *Fisher v Bell* [1961] 1 QB 394, ¶3; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶2; *Partridge v Crittenden* [1968] 1 WLR 1204, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶2; Fact sheet ¶3
- **C2.3** [I3] *(replies to A2.3)* Skyline is entitled to retain the deposit and claim the balance because the contract was not frustrated, and the hardship without impossibility does not discharge obligations or justify repayment of deposits, consistent with Herne Bay and Davis Contractors where the risk of loss fell on the party undertaking the venture (J38:p3, J39:p2, J39:p3, F6). — *Herne Bay Steam Boat Co v Hutton* [1903] 2 KB 683, ¶3; *Davis Contractors Ltd v Fareham Urban District Council* [1956] AC 696, ¶2; *Davis Contractors Ltd v Fareham Urban District Council* [1956] AC 696, ¶3; Fact sheet ¶6
