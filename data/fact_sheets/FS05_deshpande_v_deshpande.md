# Fact Sheet FS05: Deshpande v Deshpande

Matter: A daughter's claim to a coparcenary share in joint family property
Acting for: Plaintiff
Jurisdiction: India
Practice area: Property and land

## Parties
- Plaintiff: Anjali Deshpande, daughter of the late Prakash Deshpande.
- Defendants: her brothers Rohit and Sanjay Deshpande.

## Facts
1. Prakash Deshpande and his two sons formed a Hindu Undivided Family that owned a house in Pune and 6 acres of ancestral agricultural land in Satara, Maharashtra.
2. The plaintiff was born in 1978 and married in 2001. Her father died intestate on 3 February 2004.
3. There was no registered partition and no partition decree. The brothers say there was an oral family partition in 2003 under which the plaintiff received jewellery at her marriage and nothing else.
4. In 2023 the plaintiff filed a suit for partition claiming an equal share with her brothers as a coparcener under section 6 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, as amended in 2005.
5. The brothers say the 2005 amendment does not help her because their father died before 9 September 2005, and that the oral partition of 2003 ended the coparcenary.

## Client's objective
The plaintiff wants a declaration that she is a coparcener with an equal share and a preliminary decree for partition.
