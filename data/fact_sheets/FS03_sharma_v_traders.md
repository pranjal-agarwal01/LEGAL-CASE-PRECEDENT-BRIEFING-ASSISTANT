# Fact Sheet FS03: Sharma v Bansal Traders

Matter: Eviction of a commercial tenant on the ground of the landlord's bona fide requirement
Acting for: Plaintiff
Jurisdiction: India
Practice area: Property and land

## Parties
- Plaintiff (landlord): Meena Sharma, owner of a shop on the ground floor of her building in Lajpat Nagar, New Delhi.
- Defendant (tenant): Bansal Traders, a partnership that has run a hardware shop in the premises since 1996 at a rent of Rs 3,500 a month.

## Facts
1. The landlord's son completed a pharmacy degree in 2023 and has a drug licence application pending. He has no other premises from which to run a chemist's shop.
2. The landlord owns no other commercial property in Delhi. The family lives on the first floor of the same building.
3. In 2024 the landlord filed an eviction petition before the Rent Controller under section 14(1)(e) of the Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958, saying she needs the shop bona fide for her son's business.
4. The tenant applied for leave to defend. It says the son could work from a shop the family rented out in Faridabad in 2015, that the need is a pretext to raise the rent, and that the landlord's husband once offered to let the tenant stay at a higher rent.
5. The Faridabad shop was sold in 2019. The landlord has the sale deed.

## Client's objective
The landlord wants an eviction order and wants the tenant's application for leave to defend refused.
