# Fact Sheet FS03: Hughes v PeakFit Ltd

Matter: Enforcing a promised payment in an online advertisement
Acting for: Claimant
Jurisdiction: England and Wales

## Parties
- Claimant: Megan Hughes, a 29-year-old nurse.
- Defendant: PeakFit Ltd, the company that operates the "PeakFit 100" fitness app.

## Facts
1. On 5 January 2025 PeakFit published an advertisement on its website and social media: "Complete all 100 daily PeakFit workouts by 15 April and if you have not lost 5 kg we will pay you £2,000. We have placed £100,000 with Northgate Bank to show we mean it."
2. The advertisement said participants must log every workout in the app and weigh in on the app's connected scales on 15 April.
3. Ms Hughes downloaded the app on 6 January 2025, paid the £9.99 subscription, logged all 100 workouts and weighed in on 15 April 2025, having lost only 1.2 kg.
4. On 20 April 2025 she emailed PeakFit claiming £2,000. PeakFit replied that the advertisement was a marketing promotion and only an invitation to treat, that it never accepted her as a participant, and that she should have told it when she started.
5. PeakFit's general terms say that promotions "may be withdrawn at any time". PeakFit did not withdraw this promotion before 15 April 2025.
6. More than 3,000 people downloaded the app during the promotion. PeakFit says it never expected to be legally bound to pay all of them and that the advertisement was obvious marketing puff.

## Client's objective
Ms Hughes wants to enforce PeakFit's promise to pay £2,000 as a binding contract and claim the money.
