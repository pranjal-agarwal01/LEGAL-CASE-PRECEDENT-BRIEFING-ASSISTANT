# Fact Sheet FS02: Okafor v St Aldhelm's Hospital NHS Trust

Matter: Failure to warn of a surgical risk
Acting for: Claimant
Jurisdiction: England and Wales

## Parties
- Claimant: Daniel Okafor, a 52-year-old professional violinist.
- Defendant: St Aldhelm's Hospital NHS Trust, employer of Mr Graham Lyle, consultant spinal surgeon.

## Facts
1. On 2 February 2024 Mr Okafor consulted Mr Lyle about chronic neck pain radiating into his left arm.
2. Mr Lyle recommended cervical decompression surgery. He did not mention the roughly 2 per cent risk of damage to the nerve root supplying the hand, and he did not mention the alternative of a further six months of physiotherapy and steroid injections.
3. During the consultation Mr Okafor told Mr Lyle that his career depended on fine finger control of his left hand.
4. Mr Okafor signed a consent form and the operation took place on 11 April 2024. It was performed with proper skill.
5. The risk of nerve damage materialised. Mr Okafor has permanently lost fine motor control in two fingers of his left hand and can no longer perform professionally.
6. Mr Okafor says that if he had been warned of the risk he would not have consented to surgery on that date but would have tried physiotherapy first and sought a second opinion. He accepts he might eventually have had the same operation.
7. The Trust relies on evidence from two surgeons that many responsible spinal surgeons in 2024 did not routinely mention a 2 per cent risk to patients, and argues that the surgery itself was not negligent so the failure to warn caused no loss.

## Client's objective
Mr Okafor wants to claim damages in negligence for the failure to warn him of a material risk and of the alternative treatment before he consented to surgery.
